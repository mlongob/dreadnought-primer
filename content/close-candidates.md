## Close candidates not in the 75

{card: Accumulated Knowledge}
**Accumulated Knowledge.** The other way to build the deck. Four AK turns the long game against control and the mirror into one you win on cards rather than on Freeze, and they pitch to Foil. The cost is four slots whose first copy is a two-mana Opt without the scry, in a deck whose other slots are one-mana interaction, and a weaker Freeze plan, since the AK lists win the long game on cards and play fewer Freezes; being an instant, it at least fits the draw-go turns where the deck holds mana up. And against the decks that play AK too — Psychatog and the other heavy blue control decks — your copies are significantly worse than theirs. You never want to be the one who casts the first AK, because it makes all of theirs better, and with fewer lands than they play you will often be forced to cast it to find your next land drop. Without AK the deck needs two or three Freeze to sideboard properly; this list has three. The two builds are both correct; this one trades the AK war for consistency at finding Dreadnought plus enabler.

{card: Misdirection}
**Misdirection.** The other card for the flex slot, and the one most lists play there: main in the Tide era and in the default build today, and often called the Force of Will of the format. It costs no mana, only a blue card from your hand, and it doesn't just stop their best spell, it turns it against them: a burn spell or a Swords onto one of their own creatures, a Naturalize onto their own Sylvan Library or Sphere of Resistance, a Counterspell or Force Spike onto a spell of theirs, or onto one of your own cantrips, in a counter war. Those swings win games. It needs a spell with a single target and a second target worth moving it to, and that is where it runs out: Annul can only target artifact and enchantment spells, so there is rarely a second target for it, and Duress says target opponent, so the only legal target is still you. It is nearly blank against Enchantress and Goblins and poor against Replenish. I play Brain Freeze in the slot because I value game one of the mirror more, but Misdirection is a strong choice, and the better one in a field of removal-heavy decks.

{card: Mishra's Factory}
**Mishra's Factory.** The Tide-era win condition against Stasis and Landstill, and the deck's way to buy time against aggro. It left because the Tide plan left, because it is a land that can't be returned to Daze, Thwart or Gush or be the Island Foil pitches — the colorless mana itself is no real concession, since it casts the Dreadnought — and because sixteen Islands plus two Factory was too few Islands. If Stasis comes back in numbers, it is the first card to reconsider.

{cards: Prohibit | Force Spike | Mana Leak}
**Prohibit, Force Spike and Mana Leak.** Prohibit is the first of the extra counters to look at, and very nearly a fifth Counterspell. For two mana it counters anything with mana value two or less, and kicked, for four, anything up to four: that covers most of the answers to the Dreadnought — Swords, Disenchant, Naturalize, Seal of Cleansing, Smother — and most of the threats and lock pieces that beat the deck, Sphere, Oath, Meddling Mage and Glowrider among them. It also dodges a Cabal Therapy that names Counterspell. Unlike Mana Leak it doesn't lose its value late in the game, when the opponent has the mana to pay. What it misses are the spells of five mana or more, and it costs four to reach a three- or four-drop, which is why it is a fifth counter, not a replacement for the four.

Force Spike and Mana Leak are Daze without the free mode. Force Spike is a fine third Daze in the jam build on the play. Mana Leak is a worse Counterspell against the decks that can pay and an equal one against the decks that can't; I prefer the fourth Opt, but either is a reasonable choice.

{card: Annul}
**Annul main.** Several lists have run two to four. Against Enchantress, Replenish, Stasis, Devourer, Mox decks and the mirror it is a one-mana Counterspell; against Sligh, Goblins, Tog and Moneyball it is blank. The sideboard count of three is the compromise; the moment the field is more than half artifact-and-enchantment decks, two go main.

{card: Tsabo's Web}
**Tsabo's Web main.** It cantrips, so it is close to free, and it shuts off Port, Factory, Treetop, Wasteland and Dust Bowl. Against the decks that don't run those it is a two-mana Opt. See the sideboard section for Web versus Back to Basics.

{card: Dominate}
**Dominate.** The AK build's plan B, and a good one: let them spend their resources committing a threat, then steal it and fight on that front without ever casting a Dreadnought of your own. It is great in the mirror, where the stolen Dreadnought is the game; great against Psychatog; and it takes manlands, so a Treetop Village becomes yours. The reasons it isn't here are that this list already has a plan B in Brain Freeze, and that the Treetop plan conflicts with Back to Basics or Web, which lock the land you'd want to steal. A bit situational for my liking, but a real card, and the one to look at if the Freeze plan ever stops working.

{cards: Intuition | Reality Ripple | Boomerang}
**Intuition, Reality Ripple, Boomerang.** Cards Dave Kaplan has played in his lists, and the three with the strongest case for a slot that isn't in this 60. Intuition for three Dreadnoughts or three Stifles is a tutor for whichever half of the combo you're missing, at three mana. Reality Ripple phases out an artifact, a creature or a land, so it is the Charm's protection mode plus a tempo card: fog a Terravore or a Tog for a turn, phase out their Treetop or their only black source on their upkeep. It gives up the mill and land-type modes. Boomerang is a Chain that can hit lands and can't be copied against you, at twice the cost. All three are good choices; whether one is right over the card it would replace depends on your meta and the way you play.

{card: Mage's Guile}
**Mage's Guile.** Another way to protect the Dreadnought: shroud against Swords and the other targeted answers, and a cycle for one when there is nothing to protect. It doesn't handle an Edict or a Wrath, as the Charm does, and it doesn't function as an enabler.

{cards: Morphling | Masticore}
**Morphling and Masticore.** Morphling is the coolest finisher in blue control. It is expensive to play, but once one hits the board it is great: it needs no enabler and protects itself — shroud, untap, flying, all for blue mana. It isn't here because it costs five in a deck built to cast everything for one or two. Masticore machine-guns small creatures, Mages, Lackeys, Elves and Glowriders alike, but its upkeep discard taxes the resource the deck's long game runs on, and every artifact answer in their deck already aimed at your Dreadnought hits it too.

{card: Faerie Conclave}
**Faerie Conclave.** A win condition that survives Wrath and ignores Humility, in older control-leaning lists. Same objection as Factory, with the added cost of entering tapped.

{cards: Hibernation | Submerge | Chill | Flash of Insight}

**Sideboard candidates: Hibernation, Submerge, Chill, a second Keg, a third Flash.** A second Powder Keg, for Mage and [Sphere]{Sphere of Resistance} decks. Hibernation is a one-sided reset against Elves, Terrageddon and Hermit decks; Submerge is a tempo card against green that doesn't kill anything. Chill, for Sligh and Goblins, though the four blasts already cover them. Each is a fair swap for the third Flare or the second Back to Basics, depending on the field you expect. A third Flash of Insight is a swap for the third Brain Freeze instead: it grinds better in the long matchups, and it digs out the second Freeze when you need it, so losing one to discard doesn't stall the plan.
