## Close candidates not in the 75

{card: Accumulated Knowledge}
**Accumulated Knowledge.** The other way to build the deck. Four AK turns the long game against control and the mirror into one you win on cards rather than on Freeze, and they pitch to Foil. The cost is four slots whose first copy is a two-mana Opt without the scry, in a deck whose other slots are one-mana interaction, and a weaker Freeze plan, since the AK lists win the long game on cards and play fewer Freezes; being an instant, it at least fits the draw-go turns where the deck holds mana up. And against the decks that play AK too — Psychatog and the other heavy blue control decks — your copies are significantly worse than theirs. You never want to be the one who casts the first AK, because it makes all of theirs better, and with fewer lands than they play you will often be forced to cast it to find your next land drop. Without AK the deck needs two or three Freeze to sideboard properly; this list has three. The two builds are both correct; this one trades the AK war for consistency at finding Dreadnought plus enabler.

{card: Misdirection}
**Misdirection.** A maindeck card in the Tide era and in the default build today, and narrower than it looks. Its real job is counter wars: redirecting an unrestricted Counterspell or Force Spike onto a spell of theirs, or onto one of your own cantrips. It does not redirect Annul well: Annul can only target artifact and enchantment spells, so there is rarely a second target to move it to. It does not work on Duress at all — Duress says target opponent, so casting Misdirection is legal but the only valid target is still you. Against removal it only works if they have a creature of their own to take the Swords, which is back-breaking and too situational to plan around. In the matchups where counter wars decide the game I would rather have the maindeck Brain Freeze, which is what took its slot.

{card: Mishra's Factory}
**Mishra's Factory.** The Tide-era win condition against Stasis and Landstill, and the deck's way to buy time against aggro. It left because the Tide plan left, because it is a land that can't be returned to Daze, Thwart or Gush or pitched to Foil — the colorless mana itself is no real concession, since it casts the Dreadnought — and because sixteen Islands plus two Factory was too few Islands. If Stasis comes back in numbers, it is the first card to reconsider.

{cards: Force Spike | Mana Leak}
**Force Spike and Mana Leak.** Both are Daze without the free mode. Force Spike is a fine third Daze in the jam build on the play. Mana Leak is a worse Counterspell against the decks that can pay and an equal one against the decks that can't; neither earns a slot over the fourth Opt.

{card: Annul}
**Annul main.** Several lists have run two to four. Against Enchantress, Replenish, Stasis, Devourer, Mox decks and the mirror it is a one-mana Counterspell; against Sligh, Goblins, Tog and Moneyball it is blank. The sideboard count of three is the compromise; the moment the field is more than half artifact-and-enchantment decks, two go main.

{card: Tsabo's Web}
**Tsabo's Web main.** It cantrips, so it is close to free, and it shuts off Port, Factory, Treetop, Wasteland and Dust Bowl. Against the decks that don't run those it is a two-mana Opt. See the sideboard section for Web versus Back to Basics.

{card: Dominate}
**Dominate.** The AK build's plan B, and a good one: let them spend their resources committing a threat, then steal it and fight on that front without ever casting a Dreadnought of your own. It is great in the mirror, where the stolen Dreadnought is the game; great against Psychatog; and it takes manlands, so a Treetop Village becomes yours. The reasons it isn't here are that this list already has a plan B in Brain Freeze, and that the Treetop plan conflicts with Back to Basics or Web, which lock the land you'd want to steal. A bit situational for my liking, but a real card, and the one to look at if the Freeze plan ever stops working.

{cards: Intuition | Reality Ripple | Boomerang}
**Intuition, Reality Ripple, Boomerang.** Cards Dave Kaplan has played in his lists, and the three with the strongest case for a slot that isn't in this 60. Intuition for three Dreadnoughts or three Stifles is a tutor the deck doesn't need at three mana. Reality Ripple phases out an artifact, a creature or a land, so it is the Charm's protection mode plus a tempo card: fog a Terravore or a Tog for a turn, phase out their Treetop or their only black source on their upkeep. It gives up the mill and land-type modes. Boomerang is a Chain that can hit lands and can't be copied against you, at twice the cost. All three are reasonable and none is better than the cantrip it would replace.

{card: Faerie Conclave}
**Faerie Conclave.** A win condition that survives Wrath and ignores Humility, in older control-leaning lists. Same objection as Factory, with the added cost of entering tapped.

{cards: Voidmage Prodigy | Hibernation | Submerge | Chill}

**Sideboard candidates: Voidmage Prodigy, Hibernation, Submerge, Chill, a second Keg.** Voidmage Prodigy is a counter on a body for control matchups. A second Powder Keg, for Mage and [Sphere]{Sphere of Resistance} decks. Hibernation is a one-sided reset against Elves, Terrageddon and Hermit decks; Submerge is a tempo card against green that doesn't kill anything. Chill, for Sligh and Goblins, though the four blasts already cover them. Each is a fair swap for the third Flare or the second Back to Basics, depending on the field you expect.
