## The sideboard

Fifteen cards, six roles, and nothing in it is a one-matchup bullet. The shape of it — Essence Flare as the removal suite, Back to Basics over Tsabo's Web — follows Dave Kaplan's lists, where the Flare and Back to Basics pairing came from; the stock list has since adopted Back to Basics as well, so this is now also the Spock and Luffy sideboard card for card. For reference, nearly every Mono-Blue Dreadnought sideboard carries Annul, Flare, Brain Freeze and the blasts, then Web or Back to Basics; Dominate and a second Keg are the minority choices.

### Annul (3)

{card: Annul}
The premier answer and the reason the artifact-and-enchantment matchups are winnable. It counters Dreadnought in the mirror, [Survival]{Survival of the Fittest}, Deed, [Sphere]{Sphere of Resistance}, [Oath]{Oath of Druids}, Library, Mox, Aura of Silence, Seal of Cleansing, Hidden Gibbons, Standstill, Humility, Grid, Opalescence, [Stasis]{Stasis}, Vise, Root Maze, Zombie Infestation, [Aluren]{Aluren}, Dream Halls and every artifact in a Devourer deck, for one mana. Size it by targets: one copy per three or four targets in their 75; against most of the artifact-and-enchantment decks that is all three.

### Back to Basics (2)

{card: Back to Basics}
The lock for decks built on nonbasic lands, and the answer to manlands and utility lands.

**Versus Tsabo's Web.** Most lists have played Web; the stock list has moved to Back to Basics, and so have I. Web stops lands with non-mana activated abilities (Port, Factory, Treetop, Wasteland, Dust Bowl, and the cycling lands, since cycling from a land is an activation), replaces itself, and is an artifact Charm can protect. B2B also stops painlands, Gemstone Mine, City of Traitors, Rith's Grove and Ancient Tomb, costs three, and dies to enchantment removal. Against Terrageddon (almost no basics), Devourer and the Hermit decks (none) and GAT (colors from City of Brass and Gemstone Mine) B2B is a lock, or a heavy tax where mana creatures carry them, and Web is a nuisance. Against Moneyball it turns off their Factories and Wastelands, and mono-black has little enchantment removal to answer it. Against Landstill, Replenish and Ponza the two are close. The decks where Web is better are the ones where neither card matters much.

### Blue Elemental Blast (2) and Hydroblast (2)

{cards: Blue Elemental Blast | Hydroblast}
Four blasts for Sligh and Goblins, where they counter Fireblast, Lackey, Warchief, Pyroblast and Pillar and kill Pup, Lavamancer and any goblin. The 2/2 split is for Cabal Therapy and Meddling Mage. They don't come in against decks that merely contain red: two red [Blasts]{Pyroblast} in a Terrageddon sideboard aren't worth four slots, and GR Oath Ponza's red is too clunky to hold Pyroblast mana (Mono-R Ponza, a different and rarer deck, is a blast matchup: its Stone Rains and Pillage are all red).

### Brain Freeze (2)

{card: Brain Freeze}
Two in the side plus one main gives three for the Freeze plans: mirror, Enchantress, Replenish, Landstill, Tog, Stasis, BW Control, GAT. See the Brain Freeze entry for why three.

### Chain of Vapor (1)

{card: Chain of Vapor}
The second copy for the matchups where tempo on a permanent is the plan: Mox decks, Mage decks, Negator and Specter decks, Tog pumps, Dryad. It stays out against green decks with lands to spare — Elves, the Rock — where it gets copied onto your Dreadnought. In the mirror it comes in for the Keg: after sideboarding the game is a counter war, and a one-mana instant that answers their Dreadnought on your terms is worth more than a permanent they can see coming.

### Essence Flare (3)

{card: Essence Flare}
The deck's removal: one mana, enchant a creature, +2/+0, and a −0/−1 counter at each of its controller's upkeeps. It is in the 75 because the format's best anti-Dreadnought creatures are mostly X/1s.

**What it kills.** Every X/1 on their next upkeep, and every X/2 in two. The prime targets are the X/1s that decide games: Goblin Lackey, Xantid Swarm, Hermit Druid, [Welder]{Goblin Welder} and Plaguebearer — and with them Pup, Lavamancer, Mother of Runes, Wirewood Symbiote, Priest of Titania, Deranged Hermit and Knight of Stromgald. The 2/2s are good secondary targets: Meddling Mage, which stops the one spell the game is about; Hypnotic Specter, a must-answer in every black deck, because a Specter left alone turns a three-card combo into a one-card-a-turn hand; and Withered Wretch. Don't hold back from Flaring a 2/2 because it lives a turn longer. Think of it as a Dismember (a newer card, not legal in Premodern): you pay one mana and some life — the hits it gets in with the +2 power while it dies — and the creature is gone. That is a fine trade, and the Mage or the Specter left alone costs more.

**Rules.** The −0/−1 counter goes on at the beginning of the enchanted creature's controller's upkeep, and they can respond to that trigger with the creature's own abilities — a Hermit Druid activates, a Symbiote bounces, a Tog pumps. Flare the Symbiote first, because it bounces any other Elf in response; Flare Mother the turn she lands, while she's summoning sick; and Flare doesn't stop a Hermit Druid from activating in response to its own death. Be careful Flaring a creature that pumps: the +2 power is theirs, and a pump in response to the counter keeps it alive through that turn — Tog, Shade, Mongrel and Rootwalla attack bigger and die at the end of it, while Quirion Dryad's +1/+1 counters are permanent and can outgrow the Flare.

**Flare your own Dreadnought.** The use that isn't obvious. The +2/+0 makes it a 14/12, the −0/−1 counter at your upkeep means nothing to twelve toughness, and an opponent who has done careful combat math and passed the turn at thirteen or fourteen life has made a mistake they can't take back. Games get won that way.
