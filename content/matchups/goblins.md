### Goblins

{cards: Goblin Lackey | Goblin Warchief | Goblin Matron | Goblin Piledriver}

{cards: Goblin Ringleader | Goblin Tinkerer | Siege-Gang Commander | Naturalize}

**Verdict.** Fairly even. The deck swarms the board with synergistic Goblins and starts attacking at once; the common construction now splashes green for main-deck Naturalize, which is the part that matters to you.

**What they do.** The main threat is Goblin Lackey, usually on turn one. Unanswered, it scams an expensive Goblin into play on turn two — expect Siege-Gang Commander, Goblin Goon, a Ringleader or a Warchief off the trigger — and builds a commanding board before you have done anything. Goblin Warchief is the other engine: a cost reducer that gives the others haste, it compresses the game and turns a Tinkerer into an immediate threat. Goblin Matron tutors. Goblin Piledriver is the beater, +2/+0 for every other attacking Goblin. Goblin Ringleader refuels the hand and is, with Matron, their card advantage. The cards that are problems for you specifically are the two or three main-deck Naturalizes and the Goblin Tinkerers.

A mono-red version is gaining popularity. The good news is that it has no Naturalize; in exchange expect more Tinkerers, sometimes Overload or Mogg Salvage from the side, and a faster, more aggressive posture built on Ruby Medallion. The plan against it is the same.

**What the matchup hinges on.** Two things: whether you answer the turn-one Lackey, and whether a Tinkerer ever gets to untap. A resolved Tinkerer that has lost summoning sickness is very hard to beat — a good pilot activates it in your upkeep, which makes Vision Charm a non-answer and means you need a Stifle for each activation. Don't let one come down if you can help it, and don't let them develop toward it through Warchief.

**Game one.** The goal is to commit a Dreadnought with adequate protection before their board can ignore it — attacking past it, or forcing you to hold it back as a blocker. Which enabler depends on the Tinkerer. With no Tinkerer on their side, Stifle is the better one, because a Stifled Dreadnought can block on their turn and a Charmed one is still phased out. With a Tinkerer out, Charm is the enabler and Stifle is held for its activation in your upkeep, as in the answers chapter. The untap trick is at its best in this matchup: attack for twelve, then in the second main phase cast another Dreadnought with no enabler and sacrifice the tapped one to its trigger, so you have an untapped blocker again before the final swing.

On their side, the countering priorities are specific. Counter the Matron, not the card she fetches: she is their best card plus a 1/1 body, and the body is not something to underestimate — every Goblin that attacks beside a Piledriver is three more damage a turn. Consider spending a counter on Warchief to buy time. Ringleader is worth a counter or a Stifle on the trigger to keep them in the dirt. The other Stifle targets: the Lackey trigger, Piledriver's pump so you don't take the massive hit, [Siege-Gang]{Siege-Gang Commander}'s tokens, and a fetchland when you sense a Forest is coming for a Naturalize — one of the times Stifling a fetchland is clearly right.

**After sideboarding.** Expect them to cut the cards that are weak against you — Ringleader, Sharpshooter, Gempalm Incinerator, Mogg Fanatic — and bring a lot of answers: more Naturalize and Crumble, extra Tinkerers, Overload, Mogg Salvage, Hearth Charm, Hull Breach and Threaten, with Terminate, Smother and Cabal Therapy in the black-splash lists, and the red [Blasts]{Pyroblast} to fight you on the stack. Skirk Prospector lets them develop and still hold up Blast mana, so a tapped-out board is not always tapped out. You bring Essence Flare, a fantastic answer to their one-power creatures and above all to the Lackey that arrives before you have Counterspell mana, and the four blue Blasts, the best cards in your sideboard here: stack interaction against the red Blasts and a removal spell for every creature they have, with the one exception of Piledriver, which has protection from blue and must be answered on the stack.

{cards: Hearth Charm | Hull Breach | Threaten | Skirk Prospector}

Flare, the Blasts and Daze on the play are the efficient answers to the turn-one Lackey, and that shapes the mulligan in games two and three: keep a hand with the Dreadnought and one of those answers. If you keep one that can't answer the Lackey and they have it, the game is usually not winnable — and they know how good the Lackey is against you, so some of the time they will mulligan into one.

**Sideboarding:** −1 Keg, −2 Flash, −1 Thwart, −1 Brain Freeze, −1 Peek, −1 Foil / +4 blasts, +3 Flare.

**Why these.** Keg looks right and isn't: it forces you to tap out of interaction on your own turn, it rarely catches the Lackey in time, and their curve spreads mana values widely enough that any one setting misses most of the board. Flash is too slow, Thwart's cost is wrong for a race, and Freeze is dead. Peek goes because you know what is coming. Daze stays: against an opponent who isn't playing around it, it is one of the best cards in the matchup, and it takes a Foil's slot rather than its own; if the opponent has shown they play around it, swap that Foil back in for the Daze.
