### Black-White Control

{cards: Swords to Plowshares | Wrath of God | Vindicate | Seal of Cleansing}

{cards: Duress | Gerrard's Verdict | Phyrexian Furnace | Skeletal Scrying}

{cards: Decree of Justice | Exalted Angel | Eternal Dragon | Phyrexian Arena}

**Verdict.** Slightly favorable. Game one turns on a commit turn through their answers; after sideboarding the Freeze plan turns their removal into blanks.

**What they do.** A control deck with answers on two fronts. For the Dreadnought: Swords to Plowshares, Wrath of God and Seal of Cleansing in the main deck, Disenchant in some lists, and Vindicate, a sorcery-speed answer that can just as well take an Island early and leave you short of mana. For your hand: Duress and Gerrard's Verdict. Verdict can target themselves too, discarding lands to gain three life each. Some lists play Phyrexian Furnace, which exiles your graveyard a card at a time and makes Flash of Insight — your card selection and card advantage in a long game — much worse. Some play Skeletal Scrying for card advantage and few threats: Decree of Justice tokens, Exalted Angel, and sometimes Eternal Dragon, which keeps coming back once they have the mana to return it from the graveyard.

**What the matchup hinges on.** Their answers against your plan. In game one they have plenty for the Dreadnought, and their discard picks your counters apart before the commit turn. After sideboarding you leave the Dreadnought plan behind, their removal has nothing to hit, and the game is whether you can build a Freeze turn before their threats get there.

**Game one.** Two ways to win. Overwhelm their removal and discard with a commit turn — a Dreadnought with more protection than they have answers — or grind them into a mill game with the Charms and the Freeze. The mill game is the harder one before sideboarding: they have enough threats and enough discard to overpower you eventually, and once they have the mana to recur Eternal Dragon, the long game usually goes their way. So lean toward the commit turn, and keep the mill for the games where it is clearly there.

Vindicate on an Island is a Stone Rain you can blank: return the targeted Island with Daze or Gush. Charm the Dreadnought in response to Swords or Wrath, and Stifle the Seal of Cleansing's ability.

**After sideboarding.** Go all in on the mill. With every Dreadnought out, their Swords and Wraths have no targets, and their Seals, their Disenchants and the Aura of Silence some of them bring have only the Keg. What is left of their deck is the threats, the discard and the card advantage.

They don't cast many spells and they won't fight you on the stack, so they won't build the storm for you: you have to pick the moment to build your own Freeze turn. The best one is a spell of theirs. With a hand full of permission, hold priority and counter the same spell again and again — every counter counts for the storm — and cast the Freezes on top. Duress and Gerrard's Verdict can take the counters you are holding for that turn, and Duress can take a Freeze you don't want to lose.

Hold Stifle for Decree of Justice, and Stifle the right ability: the trigger that makes the tokens, not the cycling. Eternal Dragon's return from the graveyard is an ability too, and Stifling it wastes the mana they paid for it. Very few lists play Gaea's Blessing, but when you mill, consider keeping a Stifle open for one.

**Sideboarding:** −4 Dreadnought, −2 Daze / +2 Brain Freeze, +3 Annul, +1 Chain.

**Why these.** Annul is for their value permanents: Phyrexian Furnace, Phyrexian Arena, and the occasional Jester's Cap, which can exile all three of your Freezes from your library. Late in the game consider letting an Arena resolve — it helps you empty their library — and don't counter a late Skeletal Scrying either. The second Chain is for tempo — bounce an Exalted Angel they paid to turn face up or a hard-cast Eternal Dragon, and it costs them the turn you need to set up the Freeze — or as the cheap spell that starts a Freeze chain. Keg stays: at zero it kills the Soldier tokens and a face-down Angel. All four Stifles stay, for the Decrees and the Dragon. Daze goes: late in a long game they have the mana to pay for it. Back to Basics stays in the sideboard: bring it in if you see Mishra's Factory, which most lists don't play.
