### Replenish

{cards: Replenish | Opalescence | Parallax Wave | Decree of Silence}

{cards: Attunement | Frantic Search | Intuition | Abeyance}

**Verdict.** Favorable. Game one should be very favorable. The sideboard games are closer, once their creatures and more Abeyances come in.

**What they do.** Another very powerful enchantment combo deck — it took first and second place at Lobstercon in 2026 — and it shares cards and mechanics with [Enchantress](#enchantress). It is blue-white, and it plays the sol lands — Ancient Tomb, and City of Traitors in the few lists that kept it once Parallax Tide was banned — to support a higher curve of spells. The goal is to put powerful enchantments into the graveyard and bring them all back at once with a resolved [Replenish]{Replenish}.

The engine is Attunement, a repeatable way to put cards into the graveyard while digging for Replenish and land drops. Frantic Search does two jobs: the same as Attunement, and ramping off the sol lands — it costs three, and untapping Ancient Tombs leaves them with more mana than they spent. Intuition is the third engine card: it finds three cards, you choose which one goes to their hand, and the other two go to the graveyard, where the deck wants them — so they search for three cards where every split is good for them. They also use it to dig for Replenish, for answers such as Swords to Plowshares, or for lands when they are short.

The combo is three cards on the table: Opalescence, Parallax Wave and Decree of Silence. With all three in play they have a complete lock. Decree counters any spell you cast. Opalescence makes every other enchantment a creature, so Parallax Wave can exile enchantments as well as creatures — itself included. Wave targeting itself leaves the battlefield and comes back with fresh fade counters, and that is how they reset it.

The ordering is what makes the combination so threatening. Holding priority, they activate Wave to exile one of your creatures — a Dreadnought, say — and then activate it again targeting Wave itself. The second activation resolves first: Wave leaves, and its leaves-the-battlefield trigger resolves too, returning everything it had exiled so far. Only then does the first activation resolve and exile your Dreadnought, and since the Wave that exiled it is already gone, nothing will ever bring it back. They can do this to your creatures and enchantments as many times as they like. The same tool protects their own permanents from removal, exiled in response and returned, and resets or untaps their enchantments whenever they want — all while their animated enchantments attack you every turn.

You have two ways to break it. When Wave exiles itself, Stifle the leaves-the-battlefield trigger: Wave never comes back, and neither does anything it had exiled. And when there is a single Opalescence on the battlefield, Chain of Vapor on it ends the combination. All of this is moot once Decree of Silence is on the battlefield as well: it counters the Stifle or the Chain like any other spell. The Stifle line in particular isn't one to build around: it is a game-one trick, and after sideboarding the Stifles are gone and the focus is the mill.

Being a higher-curve deck, they run plenty of cheap answers to faster threats, and that is bad news for the Dreadnought. Most lists play four Swords to Plowshares, three or four Seal of Cleansing and four Parallax Wave in the main, all fetchable with Intuition. Newer builds also play one or two Abeyance main: cast in response to the Dreadnought, it stops you from casting the enabler, and the sacrifice trigger resolves. Some lists run Mana Leak in the main — a few play four — but many play none: don't assume they are packed with permission just because you see Islands.

**What the matchup hinges on.** Their win conditions against your counters, and the clock your mill puts on them. They main-deck too many answers to the Dreadnought for jamming to be a good plan; the plan is the Enchantress one, with one big difference — Replenish has no card-advantage engine. It plays cards that are card disadvantage instead: Attunement and Frantic Search both cost them cards. Your selection and your card advantage keep you ahead of their must-answer threats.

**Game one.** Counter their win conditions: Opalescence, and Replenish when an Opalescence is in their graveyard. Counter Abeyance when you think they can combo after it. Then use the rest of your resources to sculpt your hand and to mill them.

Time the mill. Start it only once the combo is already assembled in their graveyard, or when you have a lethal mill: milling earlier puts enchantments into the graveyard for them, and helps them cast a fast Replenish. Once the mill starts, every Vision Charm goes at their library, and Brain Freeze when the storm allows.

Intuition is a card to welcome when it searches for something you can answer on the stack: three Replenish, say. Countering the one you hand them is a two-for-one, and the two copies in the graveyard are gone from their library. Consider countering the Intuition itself when it would set up the combo whichever card you give them — a Replenish and the enchantments it returns — or when you have seen that they are stuck on mana and it would find lands.

Decree of Silence matters even when it isn't hard-cast. With enough mana they can cycle it for six to counter a spell of yours: Stifle the cycling trigger. And if they reach a lot of mana, you may have to counter a hard-cast Decree, or it shuts your deck off for too long.

Don't jam the Dreadnought into Swords, Seal, Wave and Abeyance. If you hold one, keep it for a turn when they are tapped out.

**After sideboarding.** There are two philosophies. The common one brings creatures: Meddling Mage and Exalted Angel, and some lists already play a few main. The other is creatureless, bringing more Abeyances and Orim's Chant to overload your counters and jam; that is very rare nowadays, so expect creatures. Some bring Defense Grid against you too, which makes your counters cost three more on their turn — another Annul target.

{cards: Meddling Mage | Exalted Angel | Orim's Chant | Defense Grid}

The plan stays the same: counter the win conditions, and mill once the combo is in their graveyard — just sooner, because their threats now come from two directions. Counter Meddling Mage on cast when you can; Keg or Flare the ones that resolve. Keg a face-down Exalted Angel at zero when they can't pay to turn it face up in response, or counter it.

You take out the combo: no Dreadnought, and no Stifle, since the cycling Decree is its one good target here. You bring Annul as another answer to Opalescence and to their engine, Essence Flare for Meddling Mage, two more Brain Freeze to speed up the mill, and one Back to Basics against their greedy mana base: Adarkar Wastes, Skycloud Expanse and Ancient Tomb are close to half of their lands, and that hurts a deck trying to cast four-drops.

**Sideboarding:** −4 Dreadnought, −4 Stifle, −1 Peek / +3 Annul, +2 Brain Freeze, +3 Flare, +1 Back to Basics.

**Why these.** After sideboarding they have more threats and likely more Abeyances, so you compress the game by speeding up the mill: all four Vision Charms stay, and Brain Freeze goes to three. Annul counters Opalescence, Parallax Wave, Decree, Attunement, Seal and Defense Grid for one mana. Flare is for the Mage. Peek goes with the Dreadnought plan.

Daze stays. Enchantress has the mana to pay for it — Serra's Sanctum, Wild Growth, Exploration — and casts cheap enchantments; Replenish is mana-constrained, wants to cast three- and four-drops, and often taps out to do it. Keg stays for Meddling Mage at two and a face-down Exalted Angel at zero. The full counter suite stays to answer their win conditions, and Chain stays for Meddling Mage and a resolved Opalescence. A resolved Decree is beyond it: Decree counters the Chain like any other spell.
