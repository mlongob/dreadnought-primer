### Enchantress

{cards: Argothian Enchantress | Enchantress's Presence | Opalescence | Replenish}

{cards: Parallax Wave | Solitary Confinement | Sterling Grove | Seal of Cleansing}

{cards: Exploration | Serra's Sanctum | Xantid Swarm | Gaea's Blessing}

**Verdict.** Slightly unfavorable, and very close to even when played correctly. The catch is that the correct way to play it is counterintuitive for anyone new to the deck — it asks you not to do the things the deck is built to do — which is why most people feel this is a hard matchup to win. Enchantress is one of the most powerful decks in the format right now and it finishes well at large tournaments, so it is worth learning the plan properly.

**What they do.** The deck is almost entirely lands, engine pieces and enchantments; the only exceptions are a few Swords to Plowshares and the [Replenishes]{Replenish}. The engine is Argothian Enchantress and Enchantress's Presence: one on the table is a card for every enchantment they *cast* — the enchantment doesn't have to resolve, the draw is on cast — and two is two. Their early game is landing one of those, ideally two, and from there every turn buries you deeper. The mana keeps pace: Exploration for extra land drops, Wild Growth, and Serra's Sanctum, which taps for one white per enchantment and turns a board into a storm of them in a single turn.

The midgame is a lock on several fronts at once. Opalescence with Parallax Wave is a full lock on every non-shroud creature and enchantment on the board, exiled at instant speed; Solitary Confinement means you can't damage them or target them; Sterling Grove gives their enchantments shroud so you can't interact with those either. They finish by animating the enchantments with Opalescence and attacking.

**What the matchup hinges on.** Three things are wrong for the Dreadnought player at once: their card-advantage engine is better than yours, their main deck is full of answers to a resolved Dreadnought — Seal of Cleansing, Parallax Wave, Swords — and they can lock you out, or slow you down with Elephant Grass or Confinement, before the 12/12 connects twice. Fighting on all three fronts and coming out ahead is hard. Not impossible: this deck is great because when you have the nuts you can jam into anything and win, and that line is often there. But the game-one plan I'd suggest is a different one, built on the deck's one weakness — it has a single win condition.

**Game one: counter the win conditions and nothing else.** Enchantress wins with Opalescence, two to four copies in the main deck, with one or two Replenish that can return it from the graveyard — call it four win conditions on average. So counter those four and do nothing else. Hold the Keg for an Argothian Enchantress (below). Don't jam a Dreadnought. Don't counter the engine or the value spells. Let them goldfish their deck, and counter the Opalescence when it hits the stack, and the Replenish only when an Opalescence is already in the graveyard. Some lists run a single main-deck Abeyance, and a good pilot will try to overload your permission — two Opalescences in one turn, an Abeyance in front of them — so the Abeyance is the one you have to counter first when it comes.

The rest of your game is sculpting for hard counters (Daze won't cut it here, since they have the mana), making land drops, and using your end-of-turn mana to mill them: every Vision Charm goes at their library, and so does the main-deck Brain Freeze when the storm allows. The mill does two things — it takes win conditions out of their deck so there are fewer to counter, and it runs them out of cards, which a deck that draws this much does to itself anyway. Often they draw their entire library and lose. Before sideboarding, expect no Gaea's Blessing in the main deck.

One expiry date on the plan: a resolved Solitary Confinement means you can't target them, so the Charms and the Freeze have to have done their work before it lands — counter it, or Chain it.

**Interactions worth knowing.** Stifle the Seal of Cleansing or Aura of Silence sacrifice, and the Sterling Grove activation. Mill them with Charm after a Sterling Grove or an Enlightened Tutor has put a card on top, before they draw it. The same idea works proactively: Portent them first, and if an Opalescence, a Replenish or an Enchantress effect is in the top three, leave it there and Charm the four away — a win condition countered for one mana and no Counterspell. Chain a Confinement or an Elephant Grass to attack for lethal.

Powder Keg has almost no targets here, with one exception that makes it worth having: Argothian Enchantress at two, which has shroud and dies to nothing else you play. After an Opalescence resolves, Keg can also hit a slice of their board, since the enchantments are creatures now — but don't plan on that line; once Opalescence is down you are badly behind.

**After sideboarding.** They bring a lot. To beat your interaction: Xantid Swarm, which stops you casting spells on any turn it attacks — Flare kills it before it can — and more Abeyance or Orim's Chant for the same reason. More Replenish, so the threats you've already countered come back with one spell. More answers to the Dreadnought to respect the jam plan: Aura of Silence, more Swords, more [Seals]{Seal of Cleansing}. And one or two Gaea's Blessing to respect the Freeze plan.

The counter-the-win-conditions plan no longer works on its own: the extra cards overload your permission. So games two and three change drastically. Cut every Dreadnought and go all in on the mill, but fight differently — counter the enablers now, to slow their development, while the extra Freezes let you mill much more aggressively than game one allowed.

The enablers to counter are the Enchantress effects, and the reason is what the deck looks like without them. Enchantress needs its card advantage to function: with no Argothian and no Presence on the table, all it has is the selection Mirri's Guile gives it, and it slows down to a crawl — one land and one enchantment a turn, churning through anemic draws until it finds the next engine piece. Every one of those turns is a turn you spend sculpting your hand, making land drops and preparing for the next Enchantress effect down the line, so that when it comes you have the counter and the storm. Counter the engine, and the game becomes one where you have time; let it resolve, and the game becomes one where you don't.

**Solitary Confinement after sideboarding.** Post-board the plan is to mill them proactively on one big turn, and a resolved Confinement stops that cold, so the question of whether to counter it comes up every game. The answer depends on how many Enchantress effects they have out. The lock needs two to sustain itself. With one or none, let it resolve: they skip their draw step under it, so every enchantment they hold becomes either the next enchantment or a land drop, the chain of upkeep discards runs dry once the hand has turned into lands, and the Confinement is sacrificed on its own — and a Stifle on an Enchantress draw trigger in that spot makes sure they don't see the next enchantment. With two or more effects out it is a different card: the lock sustains itself and it keeps the Freeze turn off the table, and then it is something to counter on cast or Chain in earnest.

**Sideboarding:** −4 Dreadnought, −2 Daze, −2 Vision Charm, −1 Peek / +3 Annul, +2 Brain Freeze, +3 Flare, +1 Chain.

**Why these.** Annul is the extra answer to their enchantments, and its prime target is Enchantress's Presence. Flare is for Xantid Swarm. The second Chain bounces the permanent that is beating you — Confinement, Sterling Grove, a Presence. Daze goes because they have the mana to play around it; Peek goes because what is in their hand at the end of their turn rarely matters here; two Charms go because their deck empties itself fast enough that the third and fourth Charm's mill matters less than the slots, and because every Charm has to be weighed against the Blessing they now hold. The permission stays, and all four Stifles stay, for the Blessing trigger. In my experience the plan that looks wrong — not jamming, not fighting the engine — takes this from unfavorable to slightly unfavorable or even.
