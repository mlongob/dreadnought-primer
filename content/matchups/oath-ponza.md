### Oath Ponza

{cards: Sphere of Resistance | Oath of Druids | Terravore | Sylvan Library}

{cards: Thermokarst | Winter's Grasp | Stone Rain | Swords to Plowshares}

{cards: Mox Diamond | Rishadan Port | Treetop Village | Mishra's Factory}

This entry assumes the GW build, the more popular one; GR Oath Ponza and 5C Oath Ponza are covered at the end, each with its own row.

**Verdict.** Slightly favorable to even. The anti-Dreadnought builds are harder — 5C Oath Ponza is built to beat you — and GR is the easiest version to face.

**Counter Sphere of Resistance.** On sight, every game. It turns your two-mana combo into a four-mana one and makes every free spell cost a mana: paying for Gush and Foil is extremely taxing. When you can't counter it, consider paying two for Vision Charm to phase it out at their end step before your commit turn, so it isn't taxing you on the turn you go off.

**What they do.** A land destruction deck — Ponza — with mana denial on every front: Rishadan Port and Wasteland — though against you only Port matters, since Wasteland has nothing to hit — Sphere of Resistance, and six to eight land destruction spells: Winter's Grasp and Thermokarst, and Stone Rain in the red build. The popular build adds white for Swords to Plowshares, and sometimes splashes black for Vindicate, which takes the place of some of the land destruction and doubles as removal. Expect Naturalize in the main deck as well. They almost always play four Sylvan Library for card advantage and selection.

They win two ways. Manlands, seven or eight of them between Treetop Village and Mishra's Factory, and Oath of Druids into Terravore. There is no Armageddon here: Terravore grows on the land destruction spells and on Oath itself, because an Oath trigger puts every card it reveals before the creature into the graveyard, and many of those are lands. A single Oath trigger can present a very large Terravore. Don't underestimate it.

**What the matchup hinges on.** Whether you keep your mana and your free spells working while they attack both. Sphere is the card that ends games; Swords and Naturalize — and Vindicate, where they have it — answer the 12/12; Oath turns your Dreadnought into their Terravore; and the manlands are a clock that Counterspell can't touch.

**Game one.** Four things to worry about.

1. Sphere of Resistance, as above: counter it on sight.
2. Swords to Plowshares, Naturalize and sometimes Vindicate answer the Dreadnought in the main deck. Charm in response to any of them.
3. A resolved Oath of Druids gifts them a Terravore after you've gone off, because your Dreadnought is the creature that turns the trigger on. This is relatively easy to play around. Charm the Dreadnought rather than Stifling its trigger: it is phased out through their upkeep, so you get your first swing in before Oath ever triggers — and a Terravore smaller than twelve can't kill the Dreadnought in combat, though every trigger after the first makes it bigger, because the lands in the graveyards only accumulate. You can keep Charming the Dreadnought after combat, or in their upkeep with the trigger on the stack, so that it does nothing when it resolves; or Stifle the Oath trigger itself. And you can Portent them: if a Terravore is in their top three, you know exactly how many cards the trigger will mill on the way, and so exactly how big it will be, and you can choose your play accordingly.
4. Eventually the manlands establish a clock you can't easily interact with. You have tempo plays — Stifle the activation, or Charm an animated Mishra's Factory in the declare-blockers step — but they aren't long-term fixes.

**What to counter.** Spend your counters on Sphere, on Sylvan Library, and on protecting the Dreadnought; consider countering an early Terravore too, because it can snowball. You can usually let Oath of Druids resolve, unless you hold a lot of counters — the ways to play around it above are cheaper than a counter.

Gush is your best friend against the land destruction and Rishadan Port. Gush Islands that have been Ported, and Gush Islands back to hand in response to a land destruction spell. Daze does the same job: Daze the spell by returning the Island it targets, even when they have the mana to pay — the Island is back in your hand and the spell fizzles for lack of a target.

**After sideboarding.** Most pilots side out Oath of Druids for the same number of Call of the Herd: you are too agile around Oath, and they would rather stick proactive threats. They bring more answers to the Dreadnought — Crumble, Naturalize, Aura of Silence, Seal of Cleansing, Reprisal, Disenchant — the white build brings Glowrider, a second Sphere for noncreature spells, and some bring Choke, Hidden Gibbons or Hull Breach. The 5C version adds Meddling Mage from the sideboard, and Essence Flare comes in for it; see the end of this entry.

{cards: Call of the Herd | Aura of Silence | Seal of Cleansing | Reprisal}

Your default is Annul for Oath, Aura, Seal and Library — and for Sphere on cast, before anything else — and Back to Basics to punish their extremely greedy mana base and their manland plan.

**Sideboarding:** −1 Brain Freeze, −2 Daze, −1 Flash, −1 Peek / +3 Annul, +2 Back to Basics.

**Why these.** Annul has more targets here than anywhere outside the artifact-and-enchantment decks: Sphere, Oath, Aura, Seal, Library, Mox Diamond, Choke and Hidden Gibbons. Back to Basics shuts down most of their mana and their manlands at once: a Treetop Village enters tapped and never untaps, and a Factory that has been tapped stays tapped. The second Chain stays in the sideboard: they have lands to spare to copy it onto your Dreadnought, and the main-deck copy is enough for a resolved Sphere or Oath. Thwart stays, because it is at its best against a deck that attacks your lands: it counters the land destruction outright, answers Sphere and Oath on cast, and against Port its three Islands are nearly free. Brain Freeze is dead. Flash is slow, and Peek makes room; Opt stays, because when your mana is under attack you need help finding Islands. Both Dazes leave against the white build: they hold Swords mana rather than tapping out, and the slots are worth more as Annuls and Back to Basics than as a Daze plan. All four Foils stay against the white build, even though Islands are at a premium: Foil is your dedicated tool for jamming a Dreadnought through a deck full of Swords, and the density of their removal is what decides it.

Keg stays: at zero it kills Moxes, Elephant tokens, an animated Factory or Treetop and a face-down morph, at two Sphere or a Meddling Mage, at three Glowrider or a Terravore, all without touching your Dreadnought. Blue Blasts don't come in, even against the red versions: there aren't enough targets, and their red is too clunky to hold the mana for a Pyroblast anyway.

**GR Oath Ponza.** The green-red build exists mostly to support Pyroclasm. It has fewer answers to the Dreadnought in the main deck, often none beyond Naturalize, so jam earlier: Oath triggers and Sphere are your real concerns. After sideboarding it brings Naturalize, Pyroblast and Red Elemental Blast, and sometimes Hidden Gibbons.

{cards: Naturalize | Pyroclasm | Red Elemental Blast | Hidden Gibbons}

**Sideboarding (GR):** −1 Brain Freeze, −1 Flash, −1 Peek, −2 Foil / +3 Annul, +2 Back to Basics.

**Why these, against GR.** Daze stays: it counters any land destruction spell, whether or not they are tapped out, by returning the Island it targets, and it stops a turn-one Mox-Sphere on the play. Two Foils leave instead. Foil is bad against mana denial, where Islands are at a premium, and its real job — jamming a Dreadnought through a pile of removal — matters less against a build with so few answers in it.

**5C Oath Ponza.** Claire Ackerman's version stretches the mana base to all five colors to fit Meddling Mage beside Vindicate and the white answers. It is built as an anti-Dreadnought list, and it is the hardest version of the matchup. The plan is the GW one; the difference is the Mage, which comes in from their sideboard. A Mage naming the Dreadnought, or the enabler you are short of, shuts the combo off for as long as it lives: counter it on cast when you can, and bring in two Essence Flare to answer the ones that resolve. Their mana is even greedier than GW's, so Back to Basics is at its best here.

{cards: Meddling Mage | Vindicate | Essence Flare | Back to Basics}

**Sideboarding (5C):** −1 Brain Freeze, −2 Daze, −2 Flash, −1 Peek / +2 Annul, +2 Back to Basics, +2 Flare.

**Why these, against 5C.** Two Flares for the Mage. Annul drops to two and the second Flash leaves to make room: the game turns on answering the Mage early.
