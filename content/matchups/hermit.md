### Full English Breakfast

{cards: Hermit Druid | Survival of the Fittest | Volrath's Shapeshifter | Karmic Guide}

{cards: Phyrexian Devourer | Triskelion | Battlefield Scrounger | Akroma, Angel of Wrath}

{cards: Unearth | Animate Dead | Krosan Reclamation | Palinchron}

**Verdict.** Favorable. Game one is a pure race they are poorly set up for: they have almost no answers to a resolved Dreadnought, and their combo has more fragile steps than it looks. After sideboarding they bring answers, and you can play a more controlling game.

**What they do.** Full English Breakfast is a green Survival combo deck, with black for discard and reanimation and splashes for whatever its kill needs. Hermit FEB (hFEB) is by a large margin the version you will face; classic FEB (cFEB) is the older, more dedicated Survival combo deck the name comes from. This entry is about hFEB, but most of what follows about Survival applies to both.

Their goal is to resolve one of two engines. The first is Survival of the Fittest: discarding a creature finds another, so any creature card and some mana turn into every other piece, starting with Volrath's Shapeshifter, with Palinchron untapping their lands when they need the mana. The second is Hermit Druid. They play no basic lands, so a Hermit activation mills their whole library and lets them pick the order of their graveyard. Then Unearth brings back a Volrath's Shapeshifter with Karmic Guide on top of the graveyard; the Shapeshifter enters as a Guide and starts the chain. The stock list has every finish, and which one they pick depends on whether they feel it is safe to go to combat: Devourer and Triskelion, with Battlefield Scrounger putting the right cards back, kills without combat; a Shapeshifter pumped as Psychatog, then turned into Akroma, attacks in the air.

Animate Dead gives them a way round needing Guide on top. They animate the Guide itself; its protection from black knocks Animate Dead off, the Guide is sacrificed and lands on top of the graveyard, and its own trigger returns the Shapeshifter, which enters as a Guide.

If they have no Unearth in hand and the Hermit mills it, Krosan Reclamation's flashback shuffles it into the empty library for them to draw. So without a reanimation spell in hand, they usually activate the Hermit at your end step or in their upkeep, to reach their draw step without passing a turn on an empty library. With one in hand they don't need the draw, and they can activate in their main phase and go straight into it. When they activate tells you whether they are holding a reanimation spell (Shallow Grave, below, blurs this).

{card: Shallow Grave}

A few lists are trying Shallow Grave at the moment, and it opens many lines. It returns the top creature card of their graveyard with haste, at instant speed. On a Hermit Druid that has just died, the top creature card of their graveyard, it lets the Hermit activate at once. After a Hermit activation, it combos at your end step or in their upkeep, without waiting for a main phase.

Against blue decks, the Shallow Grave player can sit on the Hermit and the Grave and play draw-go, comboing when you tap out. Or they activate the Hermit on your turn and dare you to answer the Krosan Reclamation: if you counter it or mill the cards it shuffled back, the Grave usually still kills before their draw step. Either way you need two answers, one for the Reclamation and one for the Grave. So when Shallow Grave may be in their list, don't count on the sorcery-speed timing of Unearth, and don't trust a mill in their upkeep to end the game on its own.

They fight you with discard, Cabal Therapy and Duress. Mana creatures — Birds of Paradise, Wall of Roots — and Elvish Spirit Guide let them put an engine down early, on the first turn with a Spirit Guide.

{cards: Cabal Therapy | Duress | Elvish Spirit Guide | Birds of Paradise}

{cards: Wall of Roots | Psychatog | Crumble}

This is one of the most complex decks in the format to pilot and to understand, and to play well against it you need to know their lines and every point where you can interact. [Their primer](https://hfeb.guide/) is excellent and a recommended read: it shows how the deck works from the inside.

**What the matchup hinges on.** Their engines against your interaction. Every kill needs a lot of pieces to fall into place, but every one starts with an engine card and mana, and Survival needs only those and any creature card: it is close to a one-card combo, and you should be scared of it. Once an engine is down, the rest of the combo is fragile. Counterspell and Foil hit the follow-ups — a hard-cast Shapeshifter, the Krosan Reclamation, the Unearth — Stifle hits the triggers and activations along the way, and Vision Charm and Brain Freeze hit the library. The Hermit kill runs into all of that, and since a Hermit line can leave them losing to their own empty library, every piece of interaction is bad news for them. The Survival kill is the slower of the two, since it needs more mana and more setup, but it is the dangerous one: once Survival and a Shapeshifter are on the battlefield, they can combo at instant speed, in response to anything you do, and the discard to Survival is a cost, so the top of their graveyard has already changed before you can respond. Survival is the card to counter above all.

**Game one.** They usually need a few turns of setup, and they mulligan hard for Survival or Hermit Druid. Keep hands that can answer a turn-two engine — turn one with a Spirit Guide — and still put a Dreadnought down early, around their discard; those usually win. The points where the combo breaks:

- **Stifle the Hermit Druid activation.** Their library stays where it is.
- **Stifle the enter-the-battlefield triggers:** Karmic Guide's, Palinchron's untap, a Shapeshifter that has entered as one of them.
- **Stifle Animate Dead's enter-the-battlefield trigger.** It is the trigger that returns the creature, so the Karmic Guide never enters at all. Later in the line, Stifling the trigger that sacrifices the Guide works too: the Guide stays on the battlefield instead of going to the top of the graveyard, and the Shapeshifter enters as whatever is on top.
- **Mill them at the key moments.** After Krosan Reclamation has shuffled the Unearth back into a library of one or two cards, Vision Charm or Brain Freeze in their upkeep mills it before they draw — and with an empty library, they lose on the draw, unless they have a Shallow Grave. With a Shapeshifter cast from hand and a Guide or Palinchron on top of the graveyard, Vision Charm milling four usually changes the top card, and the Shapeshifter enters as something other than what they set up; without Survival, they can't fix it again.
- **Chain of Vapor on the Shapeshifter, in the combat lines.** It is at its best once they have exiled their graveyard to pump it: bounced then, they usually have no way left to win. The same line gives them chances to Cabal Therapy you several times before they attack, though, so you may have to fire the Chain earlier, at something else that disrupts them. With your Dreadnought on the battlefield, they can sacrifice a land to copy the Chain onto it; with a lethal Shapeshifter in front of you, that is a trade you take, or counter the copy.
- **Vision Charm on a Shapeshifter copying an artifact** is a narrow out. As a Devourer it is an artifact creature, so Charm can phase it out — but only when they can't respond by discarding a Triskelion to turn it into one and fire its counters at you: no Triskelion in hand, or no mana to discard it. The moment it works is in response to the Survival activation that discarded the Devourer, before they have found the Triskelion.

Watch for the slow Hermit line. With enough mana they can activate Hermit Druid on their turn, flash back Cabal Therapy once for each creature they can sacrifice, then flash back Krosan Reclamation in their next upkeep and Animate Dead their way to a kill. It gives you a turn; the mill in their upkeep is the usual way to use it, though against Shallow Grave it may not be enough on its own.

**After sideboarding.** They bring answers to the Dreadnought — Uktabi Orangutan, Naturalize or Crumble, Swords to Plowshares — more Duress, and Xantid Swarm, which stops you casting spells on any turn it attacks and so turns off your interaction while they combo; Stifle its attack trigger if you must. The Swarm is also why they favor the Scrounger finish: they attack with it, so you can't cast spells for the rest of the turn, then combo after combat. Expect them to play with a lot more patience: discard first, then answer the Dreadnought, then combo.

{cards: Xantid Swarm | Uktabi Orangutan | Naturalize | Swords to Plowshares}

Your plan changes too: keep your interaction up, and stick a Dreadnought in a window where you can still answer their engine. Swords and the artifact removal are the answers to respect; Uktabi Orangutan usually needs a resolved Survival to find it, and it can be countered on cast or its trigger Stifled.

Flare on a Hermit Druid before it has activated doesn't stop it: the counter goes on at their upkeep, and they can activate the Hermit in response. But it forces their hand. Activating in their upkeep means they need the mana to flash back Krosan Reclamation (or cast a Shallow Grave) before the draw, and to get through your Stifles, Vision Charms and counters, and they may not go for it to respect your answers. Then the Flare is removal.

**Sideboarding:** −1 Keg, −2 Daze, −2 Flash, −1 Peek, −1 Brain Freeze / +3 Annul, +3 Flare, +1 Back to Basics.

**Why these.** Annul counters Survival and Animate Dead for one mana. All three Flares come in: they kill Xantid Swarm and pressure Hermit Druid. One Back to Basics: they play no basics at all, and although their mana creatures and Spirit Guides still cast spells under it, it cuts them off from the mana a combo turn needs. Keg leaves: at one it kills Birds and Swarm but your own Dreadnought too, at two Hermit Druid and Wall of Roots, and the Shapeshifter changes mana value whenever they want. Both Dazes leave: they play around it with a land kept untapped, and an Elvish Spirit Guide in hand pays for it, so a hard counter is usually better. On the play, Daze can be worth keeping over a Foil or an Annul, since their early engine comes down before they have spare mana; the Spirit Guide is the risk you take. Flash leaves because you don't expect a game long enough to get value from the flashback. Peek leaves because knowing their hand usually isn't worth the card here. Brain Freeze leaves too: the Vision Charms are enough to mill what Krosan Reclamation shuffles back.

Chain stays: it is the only answer left to a resolved Survival and to a Shapeshifter that is about to kill you.
