### Elves

{cards: Priest of Titania | Wirewood Symbiote | Survival of the Fittest | Deranged Hermit}

{cards: Nantuko Vigilante | Llanowar Elves | Gaea's Cradle | Winter Orb}

**Verdict.** Unfavorable, and one of the matchups that often needs some luck to win. It is also one of the main reasons the mulligan chapter tells you to go to six for a Dreadnought: against this deck the Dreadnought in the opening hand is the game.

**What they do.** Snowball into a massive board with unlimited mana, fast. Two engines do it. Wirewood Symbiote untaps Priest of Titania for another burst of mana while resetting a value creature — a Multani's Acolyte for another card, a Deranged Hermit for another four Squirrels, and the Hermit is their main finisher. Survival of the Fittest finds the right toolbox creature for the moment and puts Squee, Genesis or Anger in the graveyard for value on the way. Left unchecked, the deck buries you by turn three or four. Some lists add mana denial — Winter Orb, Tangle Wire — but the recent ones lean toward the combo end.

**What the matchup hinges on.** Whether a Dreadnought lands before the snowball. Pre-board their only real interaction is Nantuko Vigilante, one or two copies and clunky to use — it has to be found with Survival, cast face down, and flipped — and the flip is a trigger, so Stifle saves the Dreadnought when it happens. If you are late to the party, their board races a 12/12 easily, or simply blocks it with enough power to kill it.

**Game one.** Jam, and don't wait for protection: you don't have that luxury here. The ideal is a Dreadnought scammed into play on turn two. Survival of the Fittest is the first counterspell target: it is the engine, and you can't beat it once it is down — counter it on sight, whatever else is in hand. Wirewood Symbiote is the next: it untaps Priest of Titania and keeps the snowball going. Two more are must-counters: any morph creature, which you assume is the Vigilante, and Deranged Hermit, for its closing power. Beyond those, let them have the dorks: your counters are for the Survival, the Symbiote, the morph and the Hermit, and the Dreadnought is for the clock.

**After sideboarding.** They bring more answers to the Dreadnought — Naturalize, more Vigilantes — so the unprotected jam gets riskier, though it stays the plan more often than you'd like. You bring three Annul, aimed at Survival and at Tangle Wire, and three Essence Flare for the dorks that matter. Sometimes killing a mana elf buys a valuable turn, but the prime target is Wirewood Symbiote: once it is on the table every Elf of theirs can be saved from your removal by bouncing it, and the Symbiote isn't an Elf, so it has to die before anything else can. The ideal game is one where the interaction slows their development enough to find protection for the Dreadnought jam. That doesn't happen very often. More often you jam an unprotected Dreadnought early and hope they don't have the answer, which is why this matchup takes some luck.

**Sideboarding:** −2 Flash, −2 Daze, −1 Brain Freeze, −1 Thwart / +3 Annul, +3 Flare.

**Why these.** Flash is too slow for a deck that kills by turn four. Freeze is dead. Daze leaves because they have far too much mana to be caught by it. Thwart leaves because the game is decided before the third Island matters. Annul is the one-mana answer to Survival and to Tangle Wire; Flare is the one-mana answer to the Symbiote, then the dorks. Keg stays, and it is very good here: at one it sweeps Llanowar Elves, Fyndhorn Elves and the Symbiotes in a single activation, which is most of their mana and their best engine piece at once — just not with your own Dreadnought on the table, unless it is phased out first (see the Powder Keg entry). Chain stays for a resolved Survival or a Winter Orb.
