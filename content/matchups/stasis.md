### Stasis

{cards: Stasis | Black Vise | Forsaken City | Chain of Vapor}

{cards: Mana Short | Root Maze | Brain Freeze | Gaea's Blessing}

**Verdict.** It depends on their build. Slightly favorable against lists without Gaea's Blessing; unfavorable against lists that run Blessing and a Brain Freeze of their own, more so after sideboarding.

**What they do.** Mostly mono-blue, and built to lock you out of the game by resolving a [Stasis]{Stasis} and keeping it on the battlefield. Under Stasis nobody has an untap step: your Islands don't untap, your Dreadnought doesn't untap, and — the one that catches people out — phased-out permanents don't phase back in, because phasing happens in the untap step.

Their main way to keep the lock is Forsaken City. Both it and Stasis trigger at the beginning of their upkeep: with the triggers on the stack they tap the City for blue mana and float it, the City's trigger resolves and they exile a card from their hand to untap it, and the floating mana pays for Stasis. They can keep that going indefinitely — the card they draw each turn is the card they exile next upkeep — and with an Island untapped beside the City they pay for Stasis and still hold two mana for a Counterspell. The City isn't their only way, either. Gush, Thwart and Daze pick up their Islands, and as long as they can make a land drop each turn, the replayed Island stays untapped to pay for Stasis at their next upkeep.

They win with Black Vise. Under the lock you eventually can't play the cards in your hand, and Vise deals one damage for each card over four at your upkeep. Even without Stasis, its real job is to keep you at four cards, which is the opposite of how you want to play this matchup.

Around the lock: Counterspell, Thwart, Daze and, in many lists, Arcane Denial, whose drawback hardly matters to them: under a Vise you will usually decline the cards. Most lists run several Chain of Vapor. They Chain their own Stasis at the end of your turn, so they get a full untap and you don't, then cast it again; Chain is also their answer to your Dreadnought, with Powder Keg in the lists that run it. Forsaken City makes mana of any color, which lets some lists splash Root Maze, a green card, for a full lock: lands and artifacts enter tapped, so once you are tapped out you can't build your way back — and a Dreadnought enters tapped too, so under Stasis it never attacks. Most lists play a Mana Short, which is very good against you: cast in response to a Dreadnought, it leaves you no mana for the enabler, and under Stasis it taps you out for good. Many lists also play a Brain Freeze in the main deck, Gaea's Blessing in the sideboard or the main, and a Cephalid Coliseum.

{cards: Arcane Denial | Powder Keg | Cephalid Coliseum}

**What the matchup hinges on.** Whether they are on the Freeze plan too. Sticking a Dreadnought is a poor plan here: they have too many answers, and once you tap out for a Dreadnought and an enabler, they have the resources to put you under Stasis, where each Dreadnought gets at most one attack. So sit back, counter their win conditions, and mill them. Against a list with no Blessing that is much easier; against one with Blessing and a Freeze of its own, they can be the better Freeze deck. You often won't know which build you face, so plan for the worst: assume they are on the Freeze plan and can answer your Freezes with a main-deck Blessing.

**Game one.** Counter Black Vise: it is what kills you here. Counter Stasis too, when you don't think you can operate from under the lock. Late in the game, with lots of untapped Islands and answers in hand, it can be fine to let a Stasis resolve; so can an early one they won't be able to support.

Once Vise is on the battlefield, you are on a clock, and it can make sense to jam a Dreadnought just to bring your hand down. When you do commit one, prefer Stifle as the enabler: a Dreadnought phased out by its own Charm doesn't phase back in if Stasis resolves, while one enabled by Stifle stays on the battlefield.

Mill them while you do the rest. Plan every Freeze as if Gaea's Blessing is in their deck: hold a Stifle for its trigger. Charming their library at their end step a few cards at a time runs the same risk of milling a Blessing, so have the Stifle ready for that too. And watch for Cephalid Coliseum: once they have threshold, they can sacrifice it to make a target player draw three, at instant speed. When a Freeze fight leaves both libraries empty, the player who draws next loses; a Coliseum lets them make you draw first, so with one on their battlefield you can't rely on timing. Mill them out while you still have at least three cards in your library, or Stifle the Coliseum activation.

What to do under Stasis:

- **Charm their Black Vise.** It stays phased out.
- **A second Dreadnought gives you another attack.** Once the first has attacked and stays tapped, cast the second and sacrifice the tapped one to its trigger. The new one is summoning sick, but it entered untapped (unless Root Maze is out), so it attacks on your next turn — no enabler needed.
- **Rebuild your mana.** Gush, Thwart and Daze pick up your tapped Islands, and each Island you replay as a land drop comes in untapped, unless Root Maze is out.
- **Chain of Vapor on Stasis** at their end step gives you a full untap step while their lands stay tapped.
- **Stifle the Forsaken City untap.** A player who sequences it right has already floated the mana for Stasis, so it doesn't break the lock this upkeep — but the City stays tapped, and next upkeep they need another way to pay. Watch every upkeep anyway: a player who taps the City at the wrong moment, or enters the upkeep with it tapped, can hand you the game.

**After sideboarding.** What they bring depends on the list, but expect them to lean toward the mill race: Annuls for your Keg and Dreadnoughts, Mana Short, extra Brain Freezes, and Gaea's Blessing in the lists that have it, usually trimming some of the lock to make room. Stasis and Black Vise can matter less in these games, and some players cast them as bait to draw out your counters. Against a list that boards into the Freeze plan, it is a mill race, and the player with more Freezes and the Stifles to protect them usually wins it.

{cards: Annul | Mana Short | Brain Freeze | Gaea's Blessing}

**Sideboarding:** −3 Dreadnought, −2 Daze, −1 Peek / +3 Annul, +2 Brain Freeze, +1 Chain.

**Why these.** The two extra Freezes make three for the mill race. Annul counters Black Vise, Stasis and Root Maze for one mana. The second Chain answers a resolved Vise or Stasis, the two cards you most need to stop; as with any Chain, they can sacrifice a land to copy it onto your Dreadnought. Vise and Stasis still matter after sideboarding even when they come down as bait. Three Dreadnoughts leave because jamming isn't the plan; the one that stays is for dumping your hand under Vise. Both Dazes leave: the games are long, and they usually have the mana to pay. Peek makes room. Keg stays: at one it kills every Black Vise at once without costing you a counter — and your own Dreadnought with them. All four Stifles stay, for Blessing, the City and their Freeze; Thwart stays, at its best against a deck that wants your Islands tapped; and the Charms stay to answer Vise under Stasis and to mill.
