### UB Psychatog

{cards: Psychatog | Duress | Accumulated Knowledge | Gush}

{cards: Smother | Vendetta | Sickening Dreams | Wonder}

**Verdict.** Favorable. Game one is the harder half, and the main-deck Brain Freeze is what keeps it close; after sideboarding, with three Freezes against a deck that plays no Stifle, it leans strongly your way.

**What they do.** A Xerox blue deck with a black splash, built very much like yours: Impulse, Opt, Portent and Gush to run the mana, Counterspell and Foil to fight on the stack. Black buys them Psychatog, Duress, and removal in Smother, Sickening Dreams and sometimes Vendetta, plus the sideboard cards. They play more lands than you do.

In the counter war they are the better control deck. Accumulated Knowledge gives them more card advantage than you have, and Duress lets them trade a one-mana spell for one of your counters before the fight starts. On the draw, expect a first-turn Duress aimed at a second-turn Dreadnought: with a Dreadnought and a single enabler in your hand, they take the enabler. They play around Stifle: they crack Polluted Delta early, or while you are tapped out.

Their plan is to cast cantrips and interactive spells until the graveyard is full, then resolve a Psychatog and attack for lethal. Tog gets +1/+1 for each card they discard and for each two cards they exile from their graveyard, so learn the Tog math: one, plus half a point for each card in their graveyard, plus one and a half for each card in their hand — and five more for each Gush they can cast, since two Islands and two cards to discard, plus the Gush itself in the graveyard, is six and a half points of power for no mana. With that count you can tell when an attack is lethal before they declare it. On a lethal attack they cast Gush in the declare-blockers step, after you have chosen your blocks, so the mana and the cards to pump arrive when you can no longer answer them by blocking. Some lists play Wonder, which gives Tog flying from the graveyard and lets it fly over your Dreadnought.

The Dreadnought dies to Smother — it is mana value one — and to Vendetta, which costs them twelve life; Charm in response to either. Many lists also play Powder Keg, and at one it kills the Dreadnought: Charm in response when they crack it.

This entry is the blue-black build. The other Tog decks — Grow-a-Tog, with or without main-deck Meddling Mage, and Esper Tog — play much more like each other than like this one: see [Grow-a-Tog](#grow-a-tog).

{cards: Powder Keg | Polluted Delta | Counterspell | Foil}

**What the matchup hinges on.** Their four win conditions against your mill. Counter the Psychatogs while you mill them. Spend the rest of your turns cantripping, sculpting your hand and making land drops, holding countermagic up to fight over a Duress or a Tog. They don't play Stifle, and few lists play a Brain Freeze of their own, so this is the mill game you play in the mirror, and most of them can't play it back. Their real answer to the Freeze is Duress, before you cast it. The games you lose are the ones where they win a counter fight over a Tog before you have milled them out.

{card: Brain Freeze}

**Game one.** You have two ways in. Early, before they have their counters up, a protected Dreadnought can take the game by itself. Or play for the mill, fighting their Psychatogs while you build to it. Most games become the second.

Duress is the card that beats the mill plan: it can take a Freeze, or the Stifle and Charm that protect a Dreadnought, out of your hand. Whether to fight over it depends on your mana and your hand. Counter it and you have spent a counter; let it resolve and they take one. Either way it costs you a counter, so the real trade is mana for information: countering it costs mana and keeps your hand hidden, letting it resolve keeps your mana open and shows them everything. A Brain Freeze in hand changes that when you are on the mill plan: now the card Duress takes is the Freeze, not a counter, and countering it is worth much more. Duress into Psychatog is their key play, so fight over the Duress when you have the mana to counter it and still fight over the Tog that follows; let it resolve when you need that mana open for the Tog. When the Freeze is the card that matters, Portent can hide it: put it second from the top, below the card Portent's delayed draw takes, and it waits in your library where Duress can't see it.

The best time to Freeze is their end step after a long fight over a win condition, because every spell in that fight counts toward your storm. Picture their turn: Duress, your Counterspell, Psychatog, your Counterspell, their Gush, their Foil — the Tog has landed, and the storm count is six. At their end step, two Freezes mill forty-five cards, which is usually lethal at that stage of the game. If they cantrip a lot at your end step instead, you can fire it there too, when the count gets you to the right number.

Know exactly what is lethal before you fire, and go well over it when you can. When you are lethal by three cards or fewer, they may counter a single copy, stay alive, untap with Tog and attack you for lethal. A partial Freeze is dangerous here. Each card it mills is half a point of power for Psychatog, so it can hand them a lethal Tog turns earlier than they would have had one; and it puts fuel in their graveyard — a Flash of Insight or Deep Analysis to flash back, a Wonder that now gives Tog flying. Fire a partial one only when Duress is about to take the Freezes out of your hand.

You can also use a Dreadnought as the storm builder on your own turn: jam it, let them fight it, and every spell in that fight — theirs as well as yours — adds to the count; when it is over, the count is high and they have spent their mana and their cards on the Dreadnought.

The Dreadnought is also bait. When you are about to go to discard, cast one rather than discard it — even with no plan to use an enabler. They will usually fight over the Dreadnought itself, and a counter spent on it is one of their cards eaten by a card you were about to lose anyway.

The answers to Psychatog itself:

- **Counterspell it on cast.** It is one of only a few threats.
- **Powder Keg.** Build it to two as soon as you can and usually leave it there: at two it answers their own Kegs, and at three it kills Tog without touching your Dreadnought. When they stick a Psychatog, add the third counter at your upkeep and crack it on their upkeep.
- **Chain of Vapor once they have pumped it to lethal size.** If the Chain resolves after they have discarded and exiled, the cards they discarded and the cards they exiled from their graveyard are gone for nothing, and Tog goes back to their hand. That sets them back a long way. They can sacrifice a land to copy the Chain onto your Dreadnought, so it is best with no Dreadnought of yours on the table, or with a counter for the copy.
- **Essence Flare is too slow.** It kills Tog only after two of their upkeeps, gives it +2/+0 until then, and they can hold Tog in hand until it is lethal, then cast it, untap and attack before Flare matters.

A resolved Dreadnought blocks Tog as long as they can't pump it past twelve power — but with Wonder in their graveyard, they can fly over it.

{card: Circular Logic}

Stifle has targets with no Dreadnought involved: their Polluted Delta on the first turns, when it is their only blue source; their Keg when it is about to kill your Dreadnought; and the madness trigger of a Circular Logic, in the lists that play it — Stifled, the Logic is never cast.

**After sideboarding.** They bring Annul and more Powder Kegs, and some bring the fourth Duress or a Diabolic Edict; they take out Sickening Dreams and some of their card draw. Against the Edict, Charm in response: a phased-out Dreadnought can't be sacrificed. Some bring Dominate or Control Magic to steal your Dreadnought, so play around them. Dominate is an instant and takes the Dreadnought for four mana, even in response to its trigger — you can't sacrifice a Dreadnought you no longer control, so they keep it (see [Phyrexian Dreadnought](#phyrexian-dreadnought)). Charm in response, or keep a counter for it. Control Magic is an Aura cast at sorcery speed: counter it. Chain of Vapor on the Control Magic gives you the Dreadnought back, but they can sacrifice a land to copy the Chain onto the Dreadnought and bounce it, so it isn't a great play. When you cast a Dreadnought as bait, don't use the enabler: Dominate in response to the trigger still takes it, but you haven't spent a Stifle or a Charm, and you have more cards for the fight that follows.

{cards: Annul | Diabolic Edict | Dominate | Control Magic}

The plan after sideboarding is the same, and now you have an incredible edge: three Brain Freezes alongside the four Vision Charms, against a deck with no Stifle. It is very hard to lose after sideboarding. The Freeze is how you win, and the Dreadnought becomes the card that makes them spend counters rather than the way you win.

**Sideboarding:** −1 Dreadnought, −2 Daze / +2 Brain Freeze, +1 Chain.

**Why these.** The two extra Freezes make three, the main plan for the sideboard games. Both Dazes leave: this is a long game, they rarely try to stick an early Psychatog, and with more lands than you they will usually have the mana to pay. One Dreadnought goes with them; three stay, with all four Stifles, as the threat that makes them spend counters and as the storm builder for the Freeze turn. The second Chain is for a pumped Psychatog.

No Annul: its only targets are their Kegs and a Control Magic, and neither touches the Freeze plan. No Back to Basics: their Deltas fetch basics, and a few Underground Rivers aren't worth a slot. No Essence Flare, for the reasons above.
