### Sligh

{cards: Jackal Pup | Mogg Fanatic | Ball Lightning | Fireblast}

{cards: Grim Lavamancer | Cursed Scroll | Pyrostatic Pillar | Sulfuric Vortex}

**Verdict.** Very favorable in game one, closer after sideboarding but still favorable. Sligh is one of the most-played decks in Premodern, fast and punishing to any stumble; you will face it constantly.

**What they do.** Mono-red, and built to take your life total to zero as fast as any deck in the format. Cheap creatures first — Jackal Pup, Mogg Fanatic (a one-damage spell on a stick), Goblin Patrol, Grim Lavamancer (which turns their graveyard into two damage at a time) — to establish a clock. Seal of Fire for two. Then a full suite of instant-speed three-damage burn, Lightning Bolt and Incinerate, Flame Rift in some lists for four to each player, and four Fireblast in nearly all, which is the spell that usually finishes you by sacrificing two Mountains. The biggest single threat is Ball Lightning, and the thing to expect is that it hits you on turn three. Some lists run Pyrostatic Pillar or Sulfuric Vortex, enchantments whose job is to compress the game. Cursed Scroll grinds, and Barbarian Rings add reach at the end.

**What the matchup hinges on.** Whether you present a Dreadnought before their burn adds up. The great news is that before sideboarding they have no way to interact with the combo at all — no artifact removal, no counters, nothing. A Dreadnought on turn two or three, even unprotected, puts the game in your favor, and the clock the Dreadnought sets is faster than theirs.

**Game one.** Jam. The hand you want is two lands, a Dreadnought and an enabler, and nothing else needs to be there. Don't spend Foil or Counterspell protecting the Dreadnought — nothing is coming for it — and save them for the big burn: a Ball Lightning, a Fireblast aimed at your face. The small spells you can take.

Stifle has more targets here than people expect: a Sulfuric Vortex trigger on your upkeep when the two life is the difference, a Seal of Fire activation, a Mogg Fanatic sacrifice when that one damage matters. In combat, remember that Jackal Pup is no blocker for the Dreadnought: it dies, and its own trigger deals the twelve damage to its controller — so their blockers are fewer than their creatures.

One caution on the first turn: a Mountain into Mogg Fanatic is also how Goblins opens, and Goblins has main-deck Naturalize. Before you commit an unprotected Dreadnought into what might be a Naturalize, get a little more information — a second card, a Peek — unless the hand gives you nothing else to do.

**After sideboarding.** This is where the matchup gets much harder, and jamming without protection stops being safe. They bring artifact destruction — three Overload in nearly every sideboard, and Mogg Salvage, a free spell that exists in their sideboard for exactly this matchup — and Pyroblast and Red Elemental Blast to fight you on the stack. Some bring Ensnaring Bridge, which you answer on the stack with a counter, after the fact with Chain of Vapor, or with two Vision Charms to attack through it twice. Most Sligh players also reshape the deck for this matchup: out go the clunky three-drops — Ball Lightning, Cursed Scroll, Sulfuric Vortex — and in come Goblin Cadets for more cheap pressure, so the sideboarded games are a stream of two-power creatures backed by Overload and the red Blasts. Some add Threaten, which steals the Dreadnought for a turn and wins the game when it resolves; treat it as a must-counter. Their answers don't attack your life total, so the sideboard games give you a little more time to sculpt a proper commit turn than game one did.

{cards: Goblin Cadets | Overload | Threaten | Pyroblast}

You bring the four blue Blasts, which counter Fireblast, Overload, Mogg Salvage, Threaten and the red [Blasts]{Pyroblast} and kill their creatures, and Essence Flare for the creatures: Jackal Pup and Grim Lavamancer are the prime targets, and Mogg Fanatic is the third — they will sacrifice it in response for the one damage, but a Fanatic left alone adds up to a lot more than one, so it has to be answered at some point.

**Sideboarding:** −1 Brain Freeze, −2 Flash, −1 Thwart, −1 Keg, −1 Peek / +2 Blue Elemental Blast, +2 Hydroblast, +2 Flare.

**Why these.** Freeze is dead against a deck that empties its hand. Flash is too slow against a clock this fast. Thwart's three Islands are the opposite of what a race wants. Keg's useful setting here is one — Pup, Fanatic, Patrol and Lavamancer all die to it — but that is the Dreadnought's setting too, and a race has no turn to spare for a two-mana artifact that can't be cracked with the Dreadnought out. Peek goes because the information isn't worth a card here: assume the burn is in the hand.

Daze stays, because they tap out, and Fireblast and Salvage are often cast with no mana open, so Daze catches them. All four Foil stay: the resource they attack is life, not cards. Chain stays for a resolved Pillar or Bridge, or a Vortex they kept. Counterspell is for Fireblast and for their answers to the Dreadnought — Overload, Threaten, a red Blast; the blue Blasts and Flare handle the rest.

The Pillar build gets the same row. Neither Annul nor Keg is in it, so the Pillar is answered on the stack — it is a two-mana sorcery-speed enchantment cast into your untapped Islands, and Counterspell, Daze or Foil all do — or by Chain once it has resolved.
