### Grow-a-Tog

{cards: Quirion Dryad | Psychatog | Meddling Mage | Mox Diamond}

{cards: City of Brass | Gemstone Mine | Fire // Ice | Sleight of Hand}

**Verdict.** Unfavorable. They have lots of answers to the Dreadnought, and Meddling Mage can name it.

**What they do.** One of the best tempo decks in the format: lots of cantrips, cheap threats that grow as they play spells and fill their graveyard — Quirion Dryad and Psychatog — and mana acceleration in Mox Diamond. Early on they control the board and disrupt you until they stick a threat, and they keep interacting once it lands. Dryad, and Nimble Mongoose in the lists that run it, get better as the game goes on; Psychatog is the finisher, and earlier in the game a blocker that holds the ground. Around the threats they play Counterspell, Daze, Fire // Ice and Swords to Plowshares. They are a Gush deck, and some builds are an Accumulated Knowledge deck as well.

{cards: Swords to Plowshares | Counterspell | Daze | Gush}

Lists vary a lot, and the Tog family has several branches. Grow-a-Tog proper is Dryad and Psychatog in a green-blue deck that splashes for its answers, and it comes with or without main-deck Meddling Mage. The lists with main-deck Mages, sometimes called 5C Tog, are the harder version for you, because of the Mages. Esper Tog, white-blue-black, plays no Dryad. It uses Mox Diamonds to bring the Mages down early, with four Swords to Plowshares, counters and card advantage, and often Gaea's Blessing in the sideboard. It is the hardest of them, and one of this deck's toughest matchups.

Beyond those, some lists don't run Mox Diamond; some run Nimble Mongoose, Call of the Herd or Armageddon — which resets the game with a threat or a Mox Diamond already on the battlefield — and a few play Winter Orb. UB Psychatog, without the green or the white, has [its own entry](#ub-psychatog). The usual tip-off that you are facing GAT is the mana base and the choice of cantrips: it is one of the few decks running Portent, Sleight of Hand or Fire // Ice alongside white and green cards; Esper Tog shows white and black with no green. For this entry, assume a list with main-deck Meddling Mage, Quirion Dryad and Psychatog; against Esper Tog the plan is the same, with more respect for Swords and the Mages.

{cards: Nimble Mongoose | Call of the Herd | Armageddon | Winter Orb}

Their biggest weakness is their mana. A four-color deck — five, counting the Fire in Fire // Ice — is hard to support in Premodern, and they fix it with City of Brass, Gemstone Mine, Flooded Strand and Mox Diamond. The mana is fragile and painful.

{card: Flooded Strand}

**What the matchup hinges on.** Meddling Mage, and their mana. The biggest threat in game one is a Mage naming Phyrexian Dreadnought; if you can't answer it, the single main-deck Brain Freeze is your out, though a slim one in game one.

**Game one.** The Freeze plan is unlikely to work before sideboarding: they can present a clock that is hard to answer quicker than you can mill them. So look for a window to jam a Dreadnought and protect it through their interaction, before a Meddling Mage comes down and before a Psychatog can grow big enough to deal with it.

{card: Powder Keg}

Powder Keg at two sweeps Dryad and Meddling Mage without touching your Dreadnought.

This is one matchup where attacking their mana is part of the plan. Stifle their Flooded Strand to keep them off white early in the game, and Chain of Vapor on a Mox Diamond sets them back too. Play around Armageddon when you can; if it is going to resolve, Gush in response to pick your Islands up.

**After sideboarding.** The game becomes mostly the mill plan, on the same terms as against [UB Psychatog](#ub-psychatog): Freezes that are lethal, not partial ones that feed their Tog. The difference is that your plan B, the Dreadnought, faces more removal than against UB Psychatog, and they have more threats to build a dangerous clock. They may bring more removal for the Dreadnought, such as Seal of Cleansing or Aura of Silence, and Gaea's Blessing against your pivot to the Freeze, so hold a Stifle for it.

{cards: Seal of Cleansing | Aura of Silence | Gaea's Blessing}

Back to Basics taxes their mana further: with it resolved, City of Brass and Gemstone Mine stay tapped, so red for Fire, black for Psychatog and green for Dryad have to come from a Mox Diamond.

**Sideboarding:** −2 Dreadnought, −1 Charm, −2 Daze, −1 Peek, −1 Foil / +2 Brain Freeze, +2 Back to Basics, +1 Chain, +2 Flare.

**Why these.** The two extra Freezes make three for the mill plan. Back to Basics strands their colors. The second Chain hits a Mox Diamond when you are on mana denial, or resets a resolved Dryad. Essence Flare is the extra answer to Meddling Mage, which it kills in two upkeeps; it is weaker on Dryad, whose counters can outgrow it, and it can't target a Mongoose. Two Dreadnoughts leave because the Dreadnought is now plan B. A Vision Charm goes rather than a Stifle, out of respect for Gaea's Blessing: every Charm aimed at their library risks flipping one. All four Stifles stay, for the Blessing and their Flooded Strand. Both Dazes leave: they play a tempo game and keep the mana to pay. Peek and a Foil make room.

**Adjust to what you see.** Because lists vary, treat this line as a starting point and change it with what they show you. If you see no Meddling Mage, you don't need the Flares. If no Gaea's Blessing turns up in game two, cut a Stifle instead of a Charm for game three. And if the Freeze plan looks weaker against whatever they are doing, keep more Dreadnoughts in.
