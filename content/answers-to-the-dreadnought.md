## Every answer to the Dreadnought, and who plays it

The first thing that surprised me about this deck was how many colors have an answer to a 12/12 artifact creature, and from which decks they come. This chapter is the census: every card in the format that interacts with the Dreadnought plan, grouped by what it does to you, with the decks that run it main or side. Where a card is in nearly every list of a deck, I say so; percentages are left to the database.

### Cards that kill it

**White.** Swords to Plowshares is in nearly every white deck's main — Terrageddon, both Oath Ponzas, Replenish, Enchantress, Landstill, BW Control, Deadguy, The Solution, White Weenie, Tribe, GAT, the five-color Tog — and it is why Vision Charm is the protection spell: Charm in response and Swords fizzles. Reprisal (destroy a creature with power 4 or more) sits in a third of Terrageddon sideboards and some Tribe, Weenie and Solution ones. Wrath of God is main in Landstill and BW Control, side in Replenish; Charm out in response.

Disenchant and Seal of Cleansing answer the artifact: Disenchant is main in nearly every Landstill and Solution list and a third of BW Control; Seal is three copies main in Replenish and Enchantress and appears in Oath Ponza, Landstill and Terrageddon. Aura of Silence is a sideboard card almost everywhere white can cast it — Terrageddon, Enchantress, GW Ponza, BW Control, Solution, Weenie — and does double duty: it makes your Dreadnought, Keg, Flare and Back to Basics cost two more, then sacrifices as a Disenchant.

Parallax Wave belongs here rather than among the cards that merely neutralize: four copies main in every Enchantress and Replenish list. With Opalescence on the table it exiles the Dreadnought for good, through the loop. Without Opalescence it exiles it until the Wave leaves play — a few turns at most, as its fade counters run out — and when the Dreadnought returns its trigger goes on the stack again, so you need a second enabler just to keep the card you already paid for. Stifle is not the answer — they have five counters and will spend another — and neither is Charm, for the same reason. Counter the Wave on cast, with Annul or Counterspell, or plan the game as if the Dreadnought is gone.

{cards: Swords to Plowshares | Reprisal | Wrath of God | Aura of Silence}

{cards: Disenchant | Seal of Cleansing | Parallax Wave}

**Black.** Edicts: Diabolic Edict is main in Moneyball, Mono-Black Clerics and Pit Rack and in half of Rock lists, with Chainer's Edict and Innocent Blood beside it; Charm in response and you have no creature to sacrifice. Smother is main in every mono-black and Rock list and in UB Tog; Snuff Out is main in most Moneyball, Clerics and Pit Rack lists (free, and nonblack is all it needs); Vendetta is a one-of in a fifth of Rock and Tog main decks. Vindicate is main in BW Control, Deadguy and The Solution. Terminate is RB Goblins' answer, a third of their main decks. Plaguebearer belongs with the repeatable disruption below but kills too: three mana a time ({X}{X}{B} at X=1), as often as they can pay, main in half of Moneyball lists.

{cards: Diabolic Edict | Smother | Snuff Out | Vindicate}

{cards: Chainer's Edict | Innocent Blood | Vendetta | Terminate}

**Green.** Naturalize is the format's most common Dreadnought answer after Swords, and it is not only a sideboard card: three copies main in three-quarters of Goblins lists, two in Terrageddon, three in GR Ponza, two in half of GW Ponza and Zoo, a fifth of Rock; from the side it is in nearly every Elves, Survival, Madness, Rock and Zoo list. Crumble, for one green mana, is the alternative in Tribe, Stompy, Hermit decks and some Ponza and Zoo sideboards.

Uktabi Orangutan is every Full English Breakfast sideboard and a third of Elves and Survival ones, and ZIMS runs it main; it is a 2/2 body you can Stifle on entry. Nantuko Vigilante is one or two copies main in nearly every Elves list and half the Survival lists; the flip is a trigger and Stifle stops it. Hull Breach is Welder Survival's and some Zoo sideboards'. Pernicious Deed, black-green, is four main in every Rock list and shows up in Oath decks; at 1 it takes Dreadnought, Keg and Charm's targets in one activation, so Charm in response or Stifle the activation.

{cards: Naturalize | Crumble | Uktabi Orangutan | Pernicious Deed}

{cards: Nantuko Vigilante | Hull Breach}

**Red.** This is the color that surprises people. Overload destroys an artifact of mana value 2 or less unkicked, and the Dreadnought is 1: it is three copies in nearly every Sligh sideboard, half of Mono-R Ponza sideboards, a fifth of Goblins and some Zoo. Mogg Salvage is free when they have a Mountain and you have an Island, which is always: half of Sligh sideboards, a fifth of Ponza, some Goblins. Pillage, artifact or land, is main in nearly every Mono-R Ponza list. Goblin Tinkerer is two copies main in nearly every Goblins list and kills a Dreadnought a turn for R once it untaps; Keg at 2 and Stifle on the activation are the answers.

{cards: Overload | Mogg Salvage | Pillage | Goblin Tinkerer}

**Colorless.** Powder Keg at 1 is the clean answer and the mirror's favorite: two main in Landstill, main in half of Tog lists and a third of Stasis lists, side in a third of Moneyball, Mono-Black Clerics and Pit Rack lists and some BW Control. Nevinyrral's Disk is Mono-R Ponza's, Mono-U control's and the UW Stiflenought's.

{cards: Powder Keg | Nevinyrral's Disk}

### Cards that neutralize it without killing it

**They take it.** Gilded Drake is the one to know: a two-mana 3/3 whose exchange is an enters-the-battlefield trigger, main in four of five UG Survival Madness lists and most Opposition-Survival lists, a fifth of generic UG Survival, and a sideboard card in Aluren and ZIMS. Stifle the trigger, or Charm the Dreadnought in response and the exchange has no target. Control Magic is in some Tog sideboards. Threaten shows up in red sideboards, Sligh and Goblins among them: it takes the Dreadnought for a turn and swings twelve at you, which ends most races. It is a red sorcery, so it comes on their turn and the blue Blasts, Daze and Counterspell all answer it on cast; or Charm the Dreadnought in response and it has no target. Dominate is the mirror's: a third of Mono-Blue Dreadnought sideboards carry it, and it is the cleanest answer to a resolved Dreadnought there is — instant speed, no trigger to fight over, and the 12/12 is now theirs and attacking you. Counter it or Charm the Dreadnought in response.

{cards: Gilded Drake | Dominate | Control Magic | Threaten}

**They tap or fog it.** Fire // Ice is main in nearly every GAT list, a quarter of Terrageddon, and some Solution, Stasis, Zoo and splash-Stiflenought lists; Ice on your Dreadnought at your upkeep costs you the attack for two mana; counter it, or Charm the Dreadnought out in response so it has no target. Whipcorder is main in White Weenie, Rebels and a third of Solution lists; Spike Weaver and Spike Feeder appear in Survival and Oath sideboards. Kor Haven prevents all damage from one attacker each turn: a fifth of Terrageddon main decks, some Landstill and Weenie. It is a land, so Annul and Chain can't touch it; Stifle the activation or B2B it.

{cards: Fire // Ice | Whipcorder | Kor Haven | Spike Weaver}

{cards: Spike Feeder}

**They prevent the damage or the win.** Solitary Confinement is two copies main in effectively every Enchantress list and in Doomsday sideboards. Worship is in White Clerics, Rebels, Weenie and Solution sideboards and some Enchantress lists: a Dreadnought alone cannot win through it, so the answer is Chain, Annul on cast, or Freeze. Humility is main in most Landstill lists. Elephant Grass (two to attack) is main in most Enchantress lists and is paid. Propaganda is in some Stasis lists. Ensnaring Bridge is Welder prison's, White Clerics', some Pit Rack's.

{cards: Solitary Confinement | Worship | Humility}

{cards: Elephant Grass | Ensnaring Bridge | Propaganda}

**They bounce it.** Chain of Vapor is main in most Stasis lists, usually several copies, three in half the Aluren lists, a one-of in a third of Tog lists, and every mirror. Waterfront Bouncer is main in half the UG Madness lists and side in most, main in a quarter of UG Survival lists, and shows up in The Solution; it is a repeatable bounce for U, so Keg at 2 or Flare it. Man-o'-War is in every Aluren list and half of Opposition-Survival. Rushing River is in most Devourer sideboards and some Frantic Storm ones, Hoodwink in a third of Devourer's, Boomerang in a quarter of Stasis main decks, Capsize in Mono-U control. A bounced Dreadnought costs you an enabler to recast; a bounced Dreadnought with the trigger on the stack costs you nothing, which is why you let a Chain resolve in response to your own trigger when you can.

{cards: Chain of Vapor | Waterfront Bouncer | Man-o'-War | Rushing River}

{cards: Hoodwink | Boomerang | Capsize}

### Cards that counter it, or the enabler

Every blue deck fights on the stack, and the fight is over three spells, not one: the Dreadnought, the Stifle and the Charm. Counterspell is four main in Landstill, GAT, Tog, Stasis, Mono-U control and every Stiflenought, three in The Solution, and in about half the UG Survival and Oath lists. Daze is in every Stasis and UG Madness list, most UG Survival, half of GAT. Mana Leak is four in Landstill, three in The Solution, and in Replenish, Mono-U control, UG Survival, Oath and some Tog. Circular Logic is four in every UG Madness list and in a quarter of Tog lists. Force Spike is Mono-U control's; Prohibit is in half the Landstill lists; Absorb in a quarter; Arcane Denial is main in Stasis and a sideboard card in Frantic Storm; Memory Lapse in the Dimir Stiflenought and The Solution.

Foil is four main in every mirror and in most UB Tog lists, and it is the counter you can't play around by waiting for them to tap out; Thwart is the mirror's, and the one that turns their three Islands into a counter at any moment. Annul, one mana for an artifact or enchantment spell, is the cheapest counter for your Dreadnought and is in nearly every Tog, Landstill and Stasis sideboard, two-thirds of Solution's, half of UG Madness's, a third of BW Control's, and every mirror. Mana Short is Stasis's main-deck card and UB Stasis's and Frantic Storm's sideboard card: it ends your turn's interaction before you take it.

Pyroblast and Red Elemental Blast are the red decks' one-mana Counterspells, and nothing more exotic than that: in nearly every Sligh, Goblins, Zoo, Ponza and Terrageddon sideboard, two-thirds of GR Ponza's, most Devourer's, and some Hermit, Welder and Survival sideboards. On a Counterspell they win the counter war; on a Stifle or a Charm with the trigger on the stack they kill the Dreadnought.

{cards: Counterspell | Daze | Mana Leak | Circular Logic}

{cards: Annul | Arcane Denial | Memory Lapse | Mana Short}

{cards: Force Spike | Prohibit | Absorb | Foil}

{cards: Thwart}

{cards: Pyroblast | Red Elemental Blast}


### Cards that stop you comboing

These are the permanents that make the Dreadnought plan uncastable rather than answering it, and they are the reason Keg, Chain, Annul and Essence Flare are in the 75.

{card: Meddling Mage}
**Meddling Mage.** Main in every Solution list, half of GAT and five-color Tog lists, some Terrageddon and Aluren; three or four from the side in most Replenish and Landstill lists, a third of GW Ponza, a quarter of BW Control. It names Dreadnought most of the time. Brain Freeze when they've read you for plan B. Counterspell or Foil when they are a combo deck trying to force their own spell through. And sometimes your removal — Chain, Keg, Flare — when the Mage itself is the plan and they mean to beat you down with it. With two Mages the usual pattern is one on the removal and one on a plan, and the second is the one that matters. Answers: Counterspell on cast, Keg at 2, Flare (two upkeeps), Chain. The timing matters more than the answer: a Mage doesn't have to die the turn it lands. It has to die before the turn you want to cast the card it names, or before it becomes a clock you can't stop, and until one of those is close it is a reason to keep the Keg climbing rather than to crack it.

{card: Sphere of Resistance}
**Sphere of Resistance.** Four main in nearly every GR and GW Oath Ponza list, two in Welder Survival sideboards, some Mono-R Ponza. Every spell costs one more, including the free ones, so the combo that costs two now costs four — Dreadnought and enabler at two each — with protection on top of that, and Daze, Foil, Gush and Thwart all cost a mana. The decks that run it are mana-denial decks already, with Port, Stone Rain, Thermokarst, Winter Orb and Tangle Wire around it, which means a resolved Sphere is not a tax you pay but the game they are trying to win: you are being asked for four mana by a deck whose plan is to keep you off three. Annul on cast, before anything else; Keg at 2; Charm or Chain it on the turn you commit, and only then.

{card: Pyrostatic Pillar}
**Pyrostatic Pillar.** Main in the Pillar build of Sligh (about a third of Sligh lists) and in some Mono-R Ponza sideboards; every spell with mana value three or less deals you two. Chain it, or Annul it on cast; Keg can't touch an enchantment.

**Mana locks.** Choke is rare (a few Enchantress and Rock sideboards), Boil rarer; both end the game if they resolve, so don't let them through. Winter Orb is main in a tenth of Elves lists and side in a quarter, main in Opposition-Survival, side in Welder: Annul, Chain. [Stasis]{Stasis} is a deck. Arcane Laboratory is in a quarter of Mono-U control sideboards.

{cards: Choke | Boil | Winter Orb | Arcane Laboratory}

**Repeatable disruption on a body.** Plaguebearer (Moneyball, Mono-Black Clerics, Pit Rack) kills the Dreadnought every turn for three mana; Flare it on sight, Stifle the activation. Whipcorder taps it. Waterfront Bouncer bounces it for U and a discard, every turn, and the Madness and Survival decks that run it have the discard outlets to make that free; Keg at 2 or Flare. Goblin Tinkerer kills it for R once it untaps, so Keg at 2 before it does, or Stifle the activation.

{cards: Plaguebearer | Whipcorder | Waterfront Bouncer | Goblin Tinkerer}

### When the answer comes

Now the timing, because knowing the answers is half of it and the other half is when they arrive, which decides how you sequence the commit turn. Most of the time, a good opponent presents an instant-speed answer — an instant, an activation, a trigger — during your upkeep, and you should expect that as the default.

Two reasons. Your commit turn is the worst time for them to fight: you may be sitting on the mana and cards of a Gush, and if answering their interaction requires you to Gush, that mana is there for the taking. Your upkeep is the best time: if your protection is Vision Charm, the Dreadnought phases out through your attack step and you lose a whole turn of damage, and whatever mana you spend in that fight is mana you don't have on your turn or on theirs. There is a cost to them — at your upkeep you are fully untapped and may have the Counterspell mana you lacked the turn before — so it is a risk they weigh rather than a law, but plan for the worst time, which is theirs to choose.

The lines follow. Prefer lines where you never have to Charm on your own upkeep. If the interaction you expect is something Stifle answers — a Deed, a Keg, a Tinkerer, a [Welder]{Goblin Welder}, a Drake trigger — then Charm is the enabler on the commit turn and Stifle is held for their upkeep activation, not the other way round: Stifle as the enabler and Charm as the upkeep answer spends the same two cards and loses you a turn of attacks. And when the opponent is tapped out and you hold both enablers, choose the one that leaves the right answer and the mana for the upkeep fight, even if that means Charming now while they can't respond rather than Stifling and finding yourself tapped below Counterspell when they untap.

{cards: Vision Charm | Stifle | Counterspell | Pernicious Deed}
