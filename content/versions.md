## Versions of the deck: builds and splashes

Four builds of the mono-blue deck exist, and three splashes. The builds first; what separates them is how they plan to win the long game.

1.  The default build: no AK, two or three Daze, Misdirection main, no main-deck Brain Freeze. This is the majority of Mono-Blue Dreadnought lists, and it is the stock list because of two Brazilian MTGO grinders, SpockVidaLoka and LuffyDoChapeuDePalha, who have top-16'd and won Premodern Challenges with it throughout 2026 — Spock took the 138-player Championship Week Finals with it — and whose exact 75 is now shared by well over a hundred registered lists. When people say "the stock list" they mean theirs.

2.  The Accumulated Knowledge build: four AK, usually with two or three Daze and no Misdirection. About a quarter of lists: a card-advantage version that wins the long game against control and the mirror on cards rather than on Freeze.

3.  The Freeze-main builds: a minority of lists put a Brain Freeze in the main deck. This primer's exact class — no AK, two or three Daze, no Misdirection, Freeze main — is a small one, and its most common 75 is Simone Fierro's European Championship winner.

4.  The jam build: Dave Kaplan's. No Brain Freeze and no plan B at all; the long game is won by resolving a Dreadnought, and every slot serves that. Reality Ripple as a fifth and sixth enabler that also phases out a blocker or a land, Intuition to find three Dreadnoughts or three Stifles, and a sideboard built on Essence Flare and Back to Basics rather than Freeze. It gives up the mill plan for consistency at the one thing the deck does best, and it is the build this list's sideboard philosophy descends from.

This primer is written for the Freeze-main build, with the Spock and Luffy sideboard, which has come round to the jam build's Back to Basics. The splashes are a different question — not how to win the long game but whether to give up the mana base to answer the deck's weaknesses — and the rest of this chapter is the case for not doing that.

The deck is a xerox deck — cantrips standing in for lands; the 17 Islands entry explains it — and that only works because every land is an Island, and because nearly every free spell in the deck is paid for with Islands.

- Daze returns an Island. Thwart returns three. Gush returns two. Foil pitches one, plus a card.

- Back to Basics, your own sideboard card, needs them all to be basic.

- Wasteland and Dust Bowl have no targets, and the opponent's Back to Basics is blank. Port still works — of the land-based denial, it is the card that does the most against this deck. Back to Basics locks a Port after one use, but a three-mana enchantment is the wrong way to fight a card that slows your first two turns; against a deck that pairs Port with many other nonbasics, it earns its slot.

{cards: Wasteland | Dust Bowl | Rishadan Port | Back to Basics}

Every nonbasic you add is a card that can't pay for Daze or Thwart, can't be the Island Foil pitches, and gives the opponent's Wasteland a target. A two-land Daze hand is a keep in the mono version and a mulligan in a two-color one.

**What the splashes buy.** White and black are the powerful splashes, because each adds proactive disruption and a second plan rather than just removal. Red makes a different deck. All three pay the same price.

*White* (Adarkar Wastes and fetches) adds Enlightened Tutor as Dreadnoughts five through eight — it also finds Keg, Web and Back to Basics — plus Swords to Plowshares, Meddling Mage as a proactive lock piece, and Disenchant or [Seal]{Seal of Cleansing} for [Sphere]{Sphere of Resistance}, Confinement and Grid. The costs beyond the mana: Mage is a second creature to protect in a deck built to protect one, and Tutor is card disadvantage in a deck that wins on resources against control.

*Black* (Underground River, Polluted Delta) adds Duress to clear the answer before the jam, Smother and Diabolic Edict for creatures, and a Psychatog pivot in the sideboard: against decks that load up on artifact removal post-board, bring in Togs and win with a creature none of it touches. The pivot needs enough black sources to cast 1UB on turn three, which pulls the base further from Islands.

*Red* (Shivan Reef) adds Fling — throw the Dreadnought at their face with the trigger on the stack, twelve damage for three mana, Dreadnought included, and no enabler needed — with Fire/Ice and Bolt for Mage, Swarm and Lackey, and Pyroblast for the mirror and blue control. This is an all-in combo deck with two kill routes rather than a splash: it gives up the control posture, the counter wars and most of the build-up plan; and its answers to Mage, Swarm and Lackey do jobs Flare and Keg already do in the mono sideboard.

The shared cost is the one above — every painland and fetch is a land the free spells can't use and Wasteland can — and one more: every non-blue spell is a card Flash of Insight can't exile, so each one you add makes the deck's flashback card advantage weaker.

The mono-blue case is that the sideboard patches the same gaps for free. Essence Flare, Keg and the Blasts handle the small creatures; Annul, Chain and Keg handle the permanents; Brain Freeze is the second plan the black version gets from Tog. None of them ask you to play a land that isn't an Island. UW and UB Stiflenought are real decks and win events; they trade the thing no other deck in the format has — a mana base the free spells run on — for cards several decks have.
