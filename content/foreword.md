## Foreword

I'm Mario Longobardi, and I've been playing Magic: The Gathering since 2000, when, as a little kid, I was given the Starter 2000 set — in Italian. A while later a close friend handed me a chunk of his collection, and with it came my first taste of constructed: Type 2, what we now call Standard. My first ever constructed deck put [Opposition]{Opposition} next to Saproling Symbiosis. I thought it was the most powerful thing in the world.

{cards: Opposition | Saproling Symbiosis}

Since then I have quit and come back to the game half a dozen times, mostly for limited rather than constructed. Then, after Covid and well into my adult years, I picked it up for good. I rebuilt my collection with the help of [Galactus](https://www.instagram.com/galactus.it/), the game store in Rome, who supported me all the way through it, and I started playing Legacy, in a game that had changed almost beyond recognition.

About three years ago I found Premodern, and I fell completely in love with it. It reminded me of the game I learned with my dad as a child. It is fun and remarkably balanced, and it has a fantastic, friendly and casual community around it. A big shout-out to the New York communities I play with every week, [90s MTG](https://www.90smtg.com/) and [the Order of the Sacred Torch](https://www.mtgnyc.com/about) — and I'm really excited that Galactus is opening a store here soon.

Mono-Blue Dreadnought was the first deck I built in Premodern. Since then I've built and played twenty or so others, and the best part of learning each one has been seeing it through the eyes of the experts who wrote primers about it. I love reading them: the Naya Terrageddon guide, the hFEB guide, Robert Luemers's Xerox Tog primer, the Stasis guide, Dr. Suiss's Replenish primer, the Burn Ward sideboard guide and more — they are all in [Resources](#resources), and many of the ideas in these pages started there. What always made me a little sad was that the Dreadnought had no primer like them. The closest thing was Dvraska's Blue Shrimp primer, the deck's bible in the Parallax Tide era, which went stale when Tide was banned.

So this is my attempt to write everything I know about the deck down for others to read. I've played it for years, mostly casually, though I'm going to try to make it to Duress Fest in 2027 — a tall ask for the father of a nine-month-old. What I know, I learned from some of the best players of the deck: across the table, on streams and in videos. It isn't only my own games, either. The numbers in these pages come from a database I keep of every tournament list registered since the Parallax Tide ban, nearly seventeen thousand of them; [Resources](#resources) says where they come from. And the sideboard plans owe a lot to The Shrimp, the Premodern Dreadnought Discord, whose players discussed them with me and helped me review the first drafts.

I wrote the first version of this primer with the help of Claude Code. It interviewed me at length, turned a very long brain dump into the chapters you're reading, and drafted the text from it. I reviewed and corrected every section, and every practice puzzle was checked line by line by independent AI reviewers. The mistakes that remain are mine.

**Where to start.** If you're new to the deck, read [the combo and the glossary](#what-this-deck-is) in the next chapter, then [How the deck plays](#how-the-deck-plays) and [Mulligans](#mulligans), and finish with the [practice puzzles](#practice). If you already play it, start with the [puzzles](#practice) to see how you do, then dig into the [card entries](#card-analysis-combo) and the [matchups](#matchups), and keep the census of [answers to the Dreadnought](#answers-to-the-dreadnought) as a reference.

This is a living document. I'm not the best pilot of the deck — I'm not even close — and there are certainly places where I'm wrong. When you find one, start a conversation about it — I'm Flario on [The Shrimp](https://discord.gg/7g6nvher9H), or [open an issue](https://github.com/mlongob/dreadnought-primer/issues/new) — so that the primer keeps getting better.
