## Mulligans

In the blind, the hand I want has four pieces: two land drops, a Dreadnought, an enabler, and protection. I'll mulligan to find three of the four, and I want the Dreadnought to be one of them — unless the hand has the cards to find it. With the mana and an enabler already in place, two or three cantrips — or Gush and Flash of Insight — stand in for the missing Dreadnought: trust them to find it.

**What to look for.** In the blind I mulligan to hands that can present a Dreadnought on turn two or three. Not every one of them has a Dreadnought in it — the cantrips stand in — but every one can get there. The reason is game one. Against the linear decks — Sligh, Goblins, Elves — an early Dreadnought is close to a free win, because they have nothing for it before sideboarding. Lose that game, and the next two are played through all the answers they bring in.

Dreadnought is the hardest card in the deck to find. Four copies, and the eleven cantrips look at one to four cards each; a hand without one and without the cantrips to dig for it spends turns doing nothing, and most of the field kills you in that time. Keeping a seven with lands and cantrips and no threat feels safe and loses more games than a mulligan to five with a Dreadnought in it — many of this deck's losses are games spent digging for the Dreadnought that never arrived. The London mulligan is the tool for that: seeing a fresh seven and bottoming one costs far less than those games cost, and it is cheapest of all in the matchups where the only way to win is to present a Dreadnought in the first two or three turns. So in the blind, mulligan to the Dreadnought, or to the cantrips that will find it.

**What to bottom.** After a mulligan you see seven and put one on the bottom, two after a second mulligan. Bottom in roughly this order: a land beyond the third; a utility one-of the hand has no use for yet — Keg, Chain, Brain Freeze; a second Dreadnought when the hand has one enabler; a Foil with nothing to pitch to it. Remember what you bottomed: it is the first card of the bottom of your library you will arrange later.

Once you know who you're playing, the hand changes. Against anything aggressive — Sligh, Goblins, Elves, Zoo — the race is the whole game: keep Dreadnought plus enabler plus two lands and let them have the information, and mulligan a seven of counters and cantrips. Against control and combo, a hand of lands, cantrips and counters is a keep; the Dreadnought comes later and the hand builds toward the commit turn.

Against discard — Moneyball, Deadguy, the Rock, BW Control, anything with Duress and Cabal Therapy — keep seven as often as you can, because cards are the resource under attack and a seven is two more than a five. All the hand needs is lands and spells. A seven with two Dreadnoughts is often a worse hand there than a seven with none: the game starts with Therapy naming Dreadnought and you are on five cards before you've played a spell. In those matchups I'd rather find the Dreadnought later, when the discard has been spent and I'm ready to jam it, than hold it through their turn two.

Two-land hands with a Daze or a Gush are keeps: Gush turns the two Islands into a land drop and two cards, and Daze protects a turn-two jam without needing a third land.

**Example hands.** Sample sevens, each with the verdict in the blind — keep or mulligan, and where it is close I say so; once you know the matchup, the exceptions above take over. The one-land hands follow the table in the mana-base chapter; the rest follow the four-pieces rule. Each hand comes with its plan — the goldfish sequence you are keeping it for — and with what you are hoping the cantrips find, because a keep is a plan, not a collection of cards. Several of the mulligans are marked as keeps at six: if the seven you see after a mulligan looks like this, bottoming its weakest card leaves a reasonable six. The two perfect hands come first, to show what the deck is trying to draw.

{hand: Peek | Island | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Daze | Island | Stifle | Opt}

*Keep, play or draw: the perfect jam hand.* Two drops, the Dreadnought, the enabler, Daze as the interaction and Peek to see whether the coast is clear before you commit. Plan: Island, Peek them at their end step; if the coast is clear, Island, Dreadnought with Stifle on the trigger and Daze up for their answer. If it isn't, use the Opt to dig — for a Vision Charm, a Foil or a Counterspell, the protection that makes the jam safe — while making the land drops, and go the turn the hand is complete. The cantrips are looking for protection, because the hand already has the combo.

{hand: Flash of Insight | Island | Gush | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Foil | Island | Vision Charm}

*Keep, play or draw: the best hand the deck can draw.* Two drops, the Dreadnought, the enabler, and the full protection package — Gush for the Islands, Foil to pitch them to, Flash of Insight as the best card Foil can discard. Plan: Island; Island, Dreadnought with Charm on the trigger; if they answer, Gush for two Islands and Foil the answer, pitching an Island and the Flash. Anything the draws find is a bonus.

{hand: Counterspell | Island | Gush | Opt | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Island | Stifle}

*Keep, play or draw.* The same shape with a hard counter instead of the free one: two drops, Dreadnought, enabler, Counterspell, cantrip, refill. The question is whether to jam unprotected on turn two, and the answer is the matchup. In the blind you can, and it often works; against Elves or Sligh you do, because that is how those matchups are won. Against anything that holds an answer you play it slow instead: Island, Opt; Island; third land, and on turn four tap two, Gush, replay an Island, and spend the floated mana on Dreadnought and Stifle, with the two untapped lands on the table paying for Counterspell. That line needs one more land from the Opt and the draws, and the Opt is looking for it — or for a Daze or Foil, which is the same protection a turn earlier.

{hand: Daze | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Portent | Foil | Island | Vision Charm | Flash of Insight}

*Keep, play or draw.* A one-lander with Portent and a Dreadnought, which the table puts at 86% and 91% to find the second Island, and everything else a jam wants. Plan: Island, Portent. Put the Island on top so it arrives at their upkeep, and put any spell you want to keep second or third, out of reach of a Therapy or a Duress until your draw step.

Turn two, with the land in hand: play it, Dreadnought with Charm on the trigger, and the protection is already there — Daze returns one of the two Islands, and that Island with Flash of Insight is the pitch for Foil, so you have a hard counter up on a two-land board. That line is also why Daze should not be spent on a development spell of theirs that doesn't threaten the combo, however tempting the free value: here the interaction is worth more as protection than as disruption. Portent is looking for the second Island and nothing else.

{hand: Stifle | Counterspell | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Island | Opt | Thwart | Counterspell}

*Keep on the draw, mulligan on the play.* Opt alone finds the second Island 83% of the time on the draw and 71% on the play, and this hand does nothing until it does. On the draw the extra card tips it; on the play, with no Portent and no second cantrip, it goes back. When you keep it and the land arrives, the two Counterspells buy you the luxury of waiting: hold one up, counter their proactive plays, make the third land drop so Thwart is live, and combo when it is safe rather than when it is possible. Opt is looking for an Island; everything after that is looking for a second cantrip.

{hand: Foil | Island | Opt | Island | Counterspell | Impulse | Island}

*Mulligan on seven; a keep at six.* No threat and no enabler, which in the blind is the hand that feels safe and loses: it spends turns digging while the field kills it. If a six looks like this, keep it — the counters buy the turns and the cantrips find the rest — but a seven this anemic goes back for the chance of one with a Dreadnought in it. The cantrips are looking for the Dreadnought first and an enabler second, and nothing else counts until both are there.

{hand: Vision Charm | Island | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Gush | Peek | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Island}

*Keep, play or draw.* Two Dreadnoughts, an enabler, Gush and Peek. Plan: Island, Peek them at their end step; Island, Dreadnought with Charm if their hand is clear; Gush the next turn to refill, with the second Dreadnought ready if the first is answered. What the draws need to find is a second enabler, so that the second Dreadnought is a threat and not a dead card. The matchup exception from above applies to exactly this hand: against a Therapy deck the two Dreadnoughts are the liability, since Therapy on turn one names the card and takes both, and the same seven with cantrips in their place would be the better keep.

{hand: Daze | Foil | Island | Stifle | Counterspell | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Thwart}

*Mulligan, play or draw.* One land and no cantrip: 35% and 62% to hit the second Island, and a hand of counters that can't do their job on one land. The Dreadnought in it is a trap.

{hand: Gush | Island | Island | Portent | Island | Opt | Island}

*Mulligan on seven; at six it is close, and I may even go to five.* Four lands and three cantrips find the deck, but the hand has no threat and no enabler, and with this many lands a Gush only draws you past seven into discards. The lands are a resource in a long game, but as a seven this is a mulligan in the blind, and even as a six it is a hand that digs while the opponent develops. The cantrips are looking for the Dreadnought.

{hand: Opt | Vision Charm | Island | Portent | Stifle | Island | Opt}

*Keep, play or draw.* No Dreadnought, but the mana and both enablers are there, and three cantrips on two lands: Portent, Opt and Opt see seven cards by turn three, and a Dreadnought is four of the fifty-three left. An Impulse in place of one Opt would make it better still, because Impulse digs four deep for exactly the card the hand is missing. This is the hand where the cantrips are the threat, and the one card they are looking for is the Dreadnought.

{hand: Brain Freeze | Island | Stifle | Gush | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Island | Vision Charm}

*Keep, play or draw.* Dreadnought, both enablers, Gush; the Freeze is a dead card until it isn't. Plan: Island; Island; on turn three tap both Islands, Gush them back and replay one — the floated mana pays for Dreadnought and Stifle, and the replayed Island holds up the Charm for the removal. There is no cantrip in the hand, so the natural draws are the only looks, and what they need to find is a counter.

{hand: Foil | Counterspell | Island | Daze | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Foil | Stifle}

*Mulligan, play or draw.* The same trap as the one-land hand above, with two Foils in it. One land, no way to find another, and nothing in the hand does its job until it does.

{hand: Opt | Island | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Impulse | Counterspell | Island | Portent}

*Keep, play or draw.* A Dreadnought, three cantrips, a Counterspell and two lands — no enabler, but Portent, Opt and Impulse between them see eight cards by turn three, and there are eight enablers in the fifty-three. This is the ordinary good hand: the threat is in it and the cantrips are looking for the one card that turns it on.

{hand: Island | Thwart | Island | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Island | Brain Freeze | Island}

*Mulligan, play or draw.* A Dreadnought, four lands and nothing that finds the enabler: Thwart and Freeze both sit in hand doing nothing, and the hand does nothing for four turns while the natural draws look for one of eight cards. The Dreadnought is not enough on its own.

{hand: Foil | Island | Gush | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Daze | Counterspell | Island}

*Keep, play or draw.* No enabler and no cantrip, but Gush is two cards and the three counters hold the game still while the draws look for one of eight enablers. Plan: land drops, counters up, and Gush when the hand needs the cards or the land drop. Everything the draws find is wanted, but the enabler is the one that matters.

{hand: Stifle | Island | Counterspell | Vision Charm | Foil | Island | Daze}

*Mulligan on seven; a keep at six.* Both enablers and three counters and nothing to enable: no Dreadnought, no cantrip, no Gush, so only the natural draws look for one of four. A hand of answers waiting for a question; as a six it holds the game while it waits.

{hand: Opt | Vision Charm | Island | Impulse | Counterspell | Opt | Island}

*Keep, play or draw.* One enabler, three cantrips, a Counterspell, two lands. The cantrips see eight cards by turn three and are looking for one of four Dreadnoughts; the Counterspell buys the turn if they don't find it at once. Thinner than the Dreadnought-plus-cantrips hand above, because the card you need has four copies instead of eight, and still a keep.

{hand: Gush | Stifle | Flash of Insight | Island | Vision Charm | Counterspell | Island}

*Keep, play or draw.* No Dreadnought and no cantrip, but the mana and both enablers are already there, with a counter and the refill. Gush and the Flash are four cards of digging between them: trust them, and the draws, to find one of four Dreadnoughts. I would almost never send this hand back. Plan: Island; Island; Gush when the land drop needs it; Flash of Insight for X=1 once the third land is down, and from the graveyard later for the Dreadnought it is all looking for.

{hand: Gush | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Daze | Island | Stifle | Counterspell | Foil}

*Mulligan, play or draw.* The combo and the protection on one land, and Gush can't help because it needs two Islands. No cantrip means 35% and 62% to find the second land, and a hand this good on one land is still a mulligan.

{hand: Phyrexian Dreadnought | Opt | Island | Thwart | Peek | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Island}

*Keep, play or draw.* Two Dreadnoughts and no enabler on two lands, with Opt and Peek to look. The second Dreadnought is redundancy against the answer, not a second threat, until an enabler shows; Opt and the draws are looking for one of eight, and Peek is kept for the turn before the jam.

{hand: Chain of Vapor | Island | Brain Freeze | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Island | Powder Keg | Island}

*Mulligan, play or draw.* The Dreadnought with a pile of utility around it: no enabler, no cantrip, three lands and three one-ofs that answer things the opponent hasn't done yet. Nothing in it looks for the card that is missing.

{hand: Stifle | Portent | Island | Stifle | Opt | Vision Charm | Island}

*Keep, play or draw.* Three enablers, two cantrips, two lands, no Dreadnought: the mana and the enablers are established, and the cantrips dig for the one piece missing. Portent and Opt see five cards by turn two and the draws keep looking for one of four; when it comes, the hand has an enabler for it and two more for the Dreadnoughts after. Plan: Island, Portent; Island, Opt; Dreadnought the turn it arrives with a Stifle on the trigger and another in hand.

{hand: Flash of Insight | Island | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Flash of Insight | Counterspell | Island | Thwart}

*Keep, play or draw.* A Dreadnought with two Flashes and two counters and no enabler, and a plan: hold Counterspell up through the early turns, and once the third land is down, cast a Flash for X=1 at their end step each turn — three lands pay for one a turn — with Thwart online behind them. Each Flash from hand is a look now and a bigger look from the graveyard later, so the hand sees a lot of cards by the midgame while the counters keep the board quiet; the enabler is one of eight, and this hand gets to look for it.

{hand: Portent | Island | Phyrexian Dreadnought | Vision Charm | Portent | Foil | Daze}

*Keep, play or draw.* A one-lander with two Portents and the combo. The second Portent adds nothing by the turn-two drop, but the first one's 86% and 91% is the number that matters, and once the second Island lands the hand has the Dreadnought, the enabler, and Daze and Foil for protection. Plan: Island, Portent for the land; Island, Dreadnought with Charm, Daze behind it, and Foil pitching the Island Daze returns and the spare Portent.
